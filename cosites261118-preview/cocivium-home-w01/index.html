Explore the idea

Start with a real question. Map alternatives, identify assumptions, compare evidence and record why a conclusion was reached. Treat disagreement as a useful signal rather than an inconvenience.

Three ways in, each under five minutes:

  • Get oriented: CoCivium, start here in 60 seconds. What the project is and one first step.
  • Bring a question: First-use guide. A simple guide for one thing you want to improve, not a running intake service.
  • Try something offline: CoPreCursor+ demonstration, with its single HTML file to download and open locally. A rule-based example with fictional text, no AI provider and no account.

How the work evolves

Front to back: readable experiences invite thoughtful criticism. Back to front: evidence, architecture and tests inform what becomes visible. Each revision should show what changed and why.

The whole project is open: CoCivium repository README.

What we are testing

Can independent challenges and source-linked decisions improve comprehension, trust calibration and decision quality? Can feedback loops help without manipulating participants? These are hypotheses, not proven outcomes.

Two public civic-education experiments in the CoAcademy repository show the approach in practice:

  • CoRelay R0: a voluntary research and feedback onramp. Page source.
  • Civic Integrity Support R0: nonpartisan election-information literacy. Read the reviewer brief and the evidence protocol.

Read critically

Ask which claims are measured, which are speculative, what evidence could overturn them, and who is authorized to act. Feedback collection and contact endpoints are not yet represented as active.
