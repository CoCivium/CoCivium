## Replace opening with this

Nearly **$7 trillion** of global data-center capital spending may be needed through 2030. McKinsey estimates about **$5.2 trillion** of that could be required for AI workloads alone.

Gartner now forecasts **$2.596 trillion** in worldwide AI spending in **2026**, including about **$1.432 trillion** in AI infrastructure.

And on August 17, 2026, The Wall Street Journal reported that nine major technology companies carried roughly **$3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments**, mostly related to AI, on top of about **$600 billion in reported capital expenditures over their latest reported year**.

We are not starving artificial intelligence of capital.

We may be starving it of something stranger.

**Sanity infrastructure.**

Not sanity as emotion.

Sanity as the machinery that lets an increasingly autonomous intelligence establish what is real, what is current, what a symbol means, where evidence came from, who authorized an action, which future was predicted, what actually happened, and whether another intelligence can independently reconstruct the chain.

That sounds abstract until the machine can write software, negotiate purchases, route money, coordinate other agents, forecast demand, alter production, and change plans faster than the humans around it can review the consequences.

Then sanity becomes an economic variable.

And perhaps a very expensive one.

## The provocation

I am going to use a phrase that will annoy sensible people:

**AI is about world domination.**

I do not mean desire for conquest.

I mean something more mundane and potentially more important: **the economic pressure on useful predictive AI is toward progressively larger models of consequential reality.**

A small-business AI that understands only the business will eventually compete against an AI that also understands customers, competitors, suppliers, logistics, weather, regulation, energy, finance, local events, labor availability, culture, and time.

The better the second system becomes at prediction, the more of the surrounding world becomes relevant to its decisions.

The world becomes the business plan.

A June 2026 research paper proposing a "Business World Model" makes this direction unusually explicit. It describes business agents maintaining executable representations of business states, dynamics, constraints, objectives, feasible actions, uncertainty, counterfactuals, and future outcomes in order to move from instruction-following toward goal-driven planning and execution.

That does not prove autonomous businesses are inevitable.

It does show the architectural pressure clearly.

## Stronger transition into the laws

Every increase in autonomy enlarges two surfaces at once:

1. the **action surface** — what the machine can cause;
2. the **sanity surface** — what must remain recoverable about meaning, evidence, time, authority, dependency, prediction, execution, and outcome.

If the first grows faster than the second, the AI can become more intelligent while the overall system becomes less coherent.

That yields the first CoSanity Law:

> **Consequential action capacity must not scale faster than recoverable meaning, evidence, authority, dependency state, and effect verification.**

## Suggested economic framing

Do not claim that AI infrastructure becomes worthless if this problem is not solved.

Use the stronger and more defensible claim:

> **The return on AI infrastructure may be capped by the amount of autonomy that organizations can safely absorb. If intelligence requires proportional human supervision, verification, reconciliation, and liability management, compute can scale faster than usable economic agency.**

Candidate metric:

**CoSane Gap = consequential action capacity / independently recoverable consequential state**

Treat as a candidate conceptual metric, not a calibrated quantitative measure yet.

## Nonclaims

- $7T is not a claim of $7T already committed exclusively to AI.
- $5.2T is a forecast/modelled investment requirement for AI-related data-center capacity through 2030, not a booked global liability.
- $3T is not total global AI commitment; it is the Wall Street Journal's analysis of off-balance-sheet commitments across nine named technology companies, described as mostly AI-related.
- "World domination" is editorial shorthand for increasing predictive/model scope, not a claim of machine intent, consciousness, desire, or hostile agency.
- CoSane Gap is not yet an empirically calibrated metric.
- CoAll/CoSoftware/CoGibberTru remain candidate architectures unless separately proven.
