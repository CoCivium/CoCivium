# The AI CoSanity Laws: STAY SANE or Burn the Trillions?

## THE WORLD IS THE BUSINESS PLAN.

*What happens when AI gets smarter faster than its software gets sane?*

---

## We are not starving AI of money

McKinsey estimates that data centers could require about **$6.7 trillion** in cumulative capital expenditures worldwide through 2030, with roughly **$5.2 trillion** tied to AI workloads.

Gartner forecasts **$2.59 trillion** in worldwide AI spending in **2026 alone**.

And on August 17, 2026, The Wall Street Journal reported that nine major technology companies carried roughly **$3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments**, mostly AI-related, on top of about **$600 billion** in reported capital spending over their latest year.

Do not add those numbers together. They overlap.

They are not a total.

They are a warning flare.

We are already talking about capital deployment on a scale most people cannot intuitively picture.

In earlier RickPublic work, we floated an even more explosive idea: that the **direct plus indirect AI-relational capital surface through roughly 2040 could reach hundreds of trillions, perhaps around $600 trillion**.

We are not asking you to swallow that number here.

We have not yet rebuilt a publication-grade accounting basket that cleanly separates direct AI investment, dependent infrastructure, power, industrial retooling, software, automation, labor substitution and augmentation, financial exposure, capital turnover, and double counting.

So for now:

> **$600T is a hypothesis, not a fact.**

We intend to either prove it, revise it, or kill it.

The argument below does not need it.

The verified trillions are plenty.

## What do we mean by sane?

Not emotionally sane.

Not polite.

Not obedient.

Not aligned with whoever owns the server rack.

Operationally sane.

Can the system still establish:

- what an object actually is?
- which version governs?
- what was observed versus predicted?
- whether a supposedly current fact is actually historical?
- where evidence came from?
- what changed upstream?
- which downstream conclusions are now stale?
- who has authority?
- whether permission applies to this exact scope?
- whether a message was merely sent, or actually received?
- whether receipt meant understanding?
- whether understanding meant acceptance?
- whether an action occurred?
- whether the intended outcome occurred?
- whether another intelligence can reconstruct the chain independently?

Humans are spectacularly tolerant of missing structure because we patch it socially.

Five people in an office can survive on:

“Use the latest one.”

“Sarah approved it.”

“You know what i mean.”

“Push when ready.”

“Bob has the final-final version.”

Ten thousand autonomous machine processes cannot safely build civilization on Bob.

## The world is the business plan

Here is the phrase that will irritate sensible people:

**AI is about world domination.**

No, not the movie version.

No chrome skeletons.

No glowing red eye.

No secret machine parliament voting to annex Belgium.

I mean something both duller and more consequential:

> **The economic pressure on useful predictive AI is toward progressively larger models of consequential reality.**

A bakery AI that knows only the bakery will compete against an AI that also knows weather, foot traffic, ingredient markets, neighborhood events, staff availability, energy prices, supplier reliability, regulations, social trends, competitor pricing, credit conditions, transport delays, and changing customer preferences.

The better the second system becomes at prediction, the more of the surrounding world becomes relevant to its decisions.

The shop needs the street.

The street needs the city.

The city needs the economy.

The economy needs the world.

The world becomes the business plan.

A 2026 research paper titled *Business World Model* makes this direction unusually explicit. It proposes business agents that maintain representations of states, dynamics, constraints, objectives, feasible actions, uncertainty and future outcomes so they can move from instruction-following toward goal-driven planning and execution.

That paper does not prove autonomous corporations are inevitable.

It does show the pressure clearly.

And a separate 2026 enterprise benchmark found something nastier: frontier agents can miss hidden cascading side effects in enterprise workflows, creating silent constraint violations. In other words, a capable agent can act successfully at the visible layer while misunderstanding what the system will do underneath it.

## The window may have been strange, and brief

There may have been a peculiar period in AI history when small groups could work close enough to frontier capability to use general-purpose models not merely as products, but as experimental collaborators in designing the institutional software that more capable systems might later need.

That window was never free.

It required years of iteration, failure, semantic repair, memory experiments, provenance work, authority work, recovery work, and a heroic amount of learning what broke when systems became larger, faster and more relational.

The frontier is now increasingly industrial. Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports that industry produced more than 90% of notable models in 2025, while several of the most capable models became less transparent about training details. The same report also shows that open-source development remains vigorous and globally important.

So this is not “open AI is dead.”

It is a narrower possibility:

> **The cheap experimental window in which a small organization could accumulate years of frontier-adjacent institutional learning may be narrowing even while powerful open models continue to exist.**

If so, the organizations best positioned for the next software layer may not all be the organizations buying the most GPUs.

Some may be groups that started early enough to ask a different question:

> What would we build if the objective were not to capture the intelligence, crown an owner, or lock the world into one company's moat, but to let increasingly capable intelligences cooperate without losing meaning, provenance, authority, recoverability, dissent, or the humans they are supposed to serve?

Call it:

**no capture. no corruption. no crowns.**

That is not solved by publishing source code.

An open repository can still encode bad authority.

A decentralized architecture can still drift.

A formally verified component can verify the wrong formalization.

A benevolent mission statement can still become a throne.

The problem is architectural.

Can the system make capture, semantic corruption, false authority, unrecoverable state and irreversible mistakes progressively harder as it scales?

CoCivium is one candidate experiment attempting pieces of that problem.

It does not get to declare itself the answer.

It has to earn the claim by comparison.

If CoSoftware cannot demonstrate more useful autonomy, lower semantic drift, stronger recovery, fewer authority errors, better independent reconstruction and less human coordination burden than ordinary AI software, nobody should adopt it.

Including us.

## So here are the laws

The mnemonic is deliberately simple:

# STAY SANE

### **S — State must be reconstructable.**

Consequential objects need recoverable identity, provenance, currentness, dependency lineage and governing status.

If nobody can reliably answer “what exactly are we acting on?”, stop pretending the rest of the stack is sophisticated.

### **T — Time must be typed.**

Observed is not historical.

Historical is not current.

Current is not predicted.

Predicted is not planned.

Planned is not authorized.

Counterfactual is not real.

The bakery story failed here.

An AI that cannot maintain these distinctions may be brilliant and still be causally dangerous.

### **A — Authority must be explicit.**

Capability is not permission.

Recommendation is not approval.

Approval is not execution authority.

Execution authority is not acceptance of the result.

The fact that a model can move money, change production, disclose information or alter infrastructure proves exactly one thing:

It can.

### **Y — Your output is not the outcome.**

A completed model response is not a world-state change.

A successful API call is not necessarily a successful business outcome.

A green dashboard is not reality.

Systems need to bind intended effects to observed effects.

### **S — Sending is not receiving.**

Emission is not receipt.

Receipt is not understanding.

Understanding is not acceptance.

Acceptance is not execution.

Execution is not successful outcome.

Email has trained humans to forget this distinction for thirty years.

Autonomous agents cannot afford to.

### **A — Action must stay bounded.**

Autonomy should expand first through effect domains that are observable, checkpointed, reversible and explicit about blast radius.

When machine-generated action becomes cheap, rollback stops being an emergency feature.

It becomes part of the primary architecture.

### **N — Natural language is not the hard gate.**

Human language is magnificent.

It is also ambiguous, contextual, cultural, compressive and frequently underspecified.

Use it to propose, negotiate, explain, teach, persuade, joke, argue and imagine.

Do not silently let “Sarah said it's fine” become the final machine authority boundary for an irreversible action.

High-consequence meaning should bind to machine-recoverable semantic objects.

This is where candidate ideas such as **CoGibberTru** become interesting, not as an AI Esperanto or a replacement for Lean, but as a possible semantic intermediate layer connecting human meaning, typed relations, proof systems, evidence, authority and runtime effects.

### **E — Evidence must survive independence.**

No model gets universal authority.

No verifier gets universal authority.

No log gets universal authority.

No memory gets universal authority.

No proof system gets to prove that the world meant what the formalization said it meant.

For a consequential action, a separate process should be able to reconstruct the governing inputs, authority, checks, execution and observed effects from durable evidence.

That is the sanity test.

## STAY SANE, compressed

**State. Time. Authority. Yield. Sending. Action. Natural language. Evidence.**

Reconstruct the **State**.

Type the **Time**.

Bind the **Authority**.

Observe the actual **Yield**.

Separate **Sending** from receipt.

Bound the **Action**.

Keep **Natural language** outside the hard gate.

Make **Evidence** survive an independent mind.

## The CoSane Gap

There is a candidate metric hiding underneath the whole article:

> **CoSane Gap = consequential action capacity / independently recoverable consequential state**

It is not calibrated yet.

Do not put it in a quarterly report.

But conceptually, it captures the risk.

If model capability, compute, tool use and agent parallelism grow faster than recoverable meaning, evidence, time, authority and effect verification, then increased intelligence produces increasing **CoSaneDebt**.

CoSaneDebt is consequential state nobody can reliably reconstruct.

At small scale, that is technical debt.

At machine speed, across financial, industrial, civic and military systems, it becomes something else.

## Regular software probably survives

Python is not going away.

SQL is not going away.

Linux is not going away.

APIs, filesystems, databases, queues, object stores, networks, Rust, JavaScript and decades of allegedly temporary enterprise code will outlive several of us out of spite.

Formal systems such as Lean should become more important, not less.

The change is in role.

Legacy software can remain the machinery.

It should stop being asked to carry, implicitly and simultaneously, the entire burden of meaning, authority, provenance, currentness, prediction, coordination and proof for intelligence operating far above human tempo.

Traditional software:

`instructions -> machine -> effects`

AI-era conventional software:

`human language -> model -> generated instructions -> machine -> effects`

Candidate CoSoftware:

`intent -> semantic state -> evidence -> authority -> prediction -> proof obligations -> bounded execution -> observed effect -> receipt -> revised relational state -> human/machine projection`

The extra middle is not bureaucracy.

It is the steering mechanism.

## The wager

Stanford's 2026 AI Index says capability is still accelerating. Industry now produces the overwhelming majority of notable frontier models. Agent performance on structured computer-use benchmarks rose sharply, though agents still fail a meaningful share of attempts. The open-model ecosystem remains important, even as the top closed model again holds a measurable performance lead.

So the intelligence race is not waiting for us to finish the software architecture.

The infrastructure money is not waiting either.

That creates an uncomfortable possibility:

> **We may be building trillion-dollar brains on software substrates designed for human-speed organizations.**

If that is wrong, good.

If conventional agent stacks, ordinary software and increasingly capable models naturally solve these problems better than CoSoftware does, CoSoftware should lose.

Publicly.

But if intelligence is scaling faster than sanity, then the missing layer may become one of the most valuable infrastructure problems nobody priced into the AI boom.
