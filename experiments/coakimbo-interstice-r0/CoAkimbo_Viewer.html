What the canary proves and does not prove

PASS | Numerical reproduction and source controls

27 deterministic tests passed, including tamper, truncated-ledger and invalid-input rejections. Geometric shadows vary with illumination direction. Dark far-field bins exist with a fully defined synthetic surface.

FAIL | Baseline: every dark detector bin is an empty valley

The simplistic baseline fails in this toy, because a detector is measuring interference at angular-frequency bins, not directly sampling empty physical valleys. This is an instructional counterexample, not a discovery of unknown physics.

HELD | Polarization, real laser lab, external independent observations

No Stokes/Mueller solver, controlled polarizer data, lab observation, independent reproduction, human first-use measurement, or deployed CoOrbWare runtime. These remain separate tests.

FAIL | Proxy inversion reverses the outcome

In a second 120-step toy policy comparison, an evidence-proxy selector wins 12/12 calibrated fixtures, but loses 12/12 when the proxy is reversed. This is a demonstration of Goodhart/sensor miscalibration risk, not a validated real-world controller.

HELD | DMT, ET, G_d and symbolic interpretations

Altered states are legitimate topics for neuroscience research, and spiritual symbolism may inspire art or philosophical questions. Neither establishes a calibrated external optical signal. ET-technosignature claims require independently verified instrumentation and exclusion of ordinary interference. No drug experiments, ET contact or external message are claimed.
