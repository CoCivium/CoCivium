# CoMarketing 101+ | Evidence-first federation hypothesis

## Discovery vs adoption

The project has multiple verified GitHub-source doors. This does **not** establish live public domains, independent reading or viral growth. A federation is only valuable if it reduces cognitive overload and produces more completed user tasks than the simpler original onramp.

## Why not let the past vanish as someone reads?

A time-evolving article can show corrected paragraphs, amended sources, and a live timeline. But changing the user's reading material mid-sentence damages comprehension and prevents honest quotation. Artificially expiring links can manufacture attention but erodes trust, reproducibility, accessibility and archival integrity. Better: freeze the initial edition, show a calm change indicator, offer a diff and allow explicit switching. `README.md` describes the correction/tombstone exception.

## Candidate editorial architecture

- `CoCanon`: reviewable versioned base article with immutable evidence and license pointers
- `CoSection`: subject-specific editorial view, not a new person or account
- `CoReaderChoice`: a current, revocable subject and cadence preference, not covert intent inference
- `CoTime`: born/observed/updated/superseded, plus durable historical version identities
- `CoChallenge`: retained counterexamples and independent reviewer comments
- `CoProjection`: Markdown, plain HTML, RSS, Substack section, public source mirror and later CoOrbWare views
- `CoReceipt`: signed or hashed publisher identity plus actual remote read-back, distinct from recipient pickup
- `CoExit`: export, unsub, disabled updates, correction, removal and verified tombstone as appropriate

## Proposed channels and distribution choices

**In order:** source-controlled canonical article; static public site once hosted/read-tested; RSS/Atom; one existing Substack publication with limited opt-in sections; targeted contributor forums where appropriate under community rules; voluntary social posts; consent-based email; developer API/schema for tools. Only scale channels after first use and quality can be independently measured.

Substack Sections are actual product functionality, but default-subscribe and copy-list settings must remain off unless someone specifically opts into those topics. Dynamic per-reader personalization is not demonstrated here; this local static prototype contains no analytics or profiles. Custom domain setup is separate DNS/payment/security work; it is not implied by a section name.

## Trillion-sized possibility space without a trillion operations

16 axes give 8.43 trillion nominal combinations. Normalise by semantic equivalence, reject unavailable or unauthorized effects, use deterministic ranking, sample small, challenge on fixed tasks, publish proof, stop when marginal information falls below cost. Combinatorics is a *search-space upper bound*, not a claim about trillions of plausible products, independent ideas or new markets.

## Bounded high-bar trial

Recruit consenting unfamiliar readers; let each try a task matched to their interests, with the original `START_HERE.md` and the new reader as counterbalanced alternatives. Record (with consent) completion, elapsed time, confidence calibration, accurate source interpretation, correction burden, accessibility, privacy concerns, frustration, voluntary next step and subsequent use. Avoid cookies or analytics by default; allow participant-supplied aggregate metrics. Stop for a material privacy problem. Reviewers should have incentives to falsify rather than promote.

## Hard no-claims

The demo cannot guarantee virality or urgency, provide unlimited tokens/models/compute, notify people between chat sessions, dynamically update a Substack article in readers' inboxes, create new publications, operate a registered domain, or deliver to every `.node` and `.site` without permission and a working transport.
