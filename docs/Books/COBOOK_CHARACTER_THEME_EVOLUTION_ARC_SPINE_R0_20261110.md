## Primary decision: people change before the glossary expands

**Proposed book-level invariant:** A new theme, speculative world, philosophical bridge, tool or media element should normally enter only when a character's already-established goal creates a need for it. The scene changes the character's choices, knowledge, relationship or consequences; that change then supplies a reason to return to the story's spine.

This is an **editorial hypothesis**, not a scientific law. Some excellent stories use episodic structures, stable/flat protagonists, ensemble development or deliberately discontinuous form. Those are alternatives to test, not mistakes to suppress.

**Storylet gate (candidate):** `ESTABLISHED_ANCHOR → CHARACTER_NEED → CONSEQUENTIAL_ENCOUNTER → CHANGED_RELATION → RECOGNIZABLE_RETURN`. If none of the five can be stated in plain language, stage the idea elsewhere or defer it. Do not automatically insert lore because a new term is available.

## Research and evidentiary strength

| Evidence level | Research or tradition | Useful inference | What it does **not** prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classical craft argument | Aristotle, *Poetics*: causally plausible action, recognition, reversal. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/poetics.1.1.html) | Arrange consequences from earlier choices rather than unrelated chronology. | No universal arc recipe or bestseller guarantee. |
| Empirical narrative comprehension | Zwaan, Langston & Graesser (1995), event-indexing: time, space, protagonist, causality, intention. [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x/) | Help readers retain recognizable who/why/where/when and causal continuity across storylets. | No validated numeric storylet-insertion threshold. |
| Empirical transportation | Green & Brock (2000), experimental work on story absorption and story-consistent beliefs. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079236/) | Affective involvement, imagery and attention are meaningful reader outcomes to evaluate. | Not proof a compelling story is true, ethical, or always persuasive. |
| Large-scale corpus | Boyd, Blackburn & Pennebaker (2020), roughly 40,000 traditional narratives: staging, plot progression, cognitive tension; no popularity association. [Science Advances](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736/) | Useful broad pattern for sequencing a narrative's orientation and tension. | Stronger formula conformity does **not** establish quality or sales. |
| Literature/pedagogy review | Children's storybooks and perspective-taking (2019), no single formula. [Frontiers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6370723) | Offer perspectives and choices rather than one compulsory moral answer. | Not guaranteed empathy transformation. |
| Alternative critical tradition | Ursula K. Le Guin, *The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction*: stories can gather relationships without a conquering hero. [Author site](https://www.ursulakleguin.com/the-carrier-bag-theory-of-fiction) | An unfinished shared map and its holders can be the story's center. | Aesthetic argument, not experimental proof; essay remains copyrighted. |

**Research ceiling:** `COMMON_PATTERN_NE_PROVEN_OPTIMUM`; `NARRATIVE_ENGAGEMENT_NE_TRUTH`; `POTENTIAL_EMPATHY_NE_GUARANTEED_CHANGE`; `MANY_STORYLETS_NE_NARRATIVE_PROGRESS`.

## Character evolution matrix (fictional, revisable)

| Character | Starting belief or need | Pressure from the story | Decision that reveals change | Theme earned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Beetle** | “I must repair the doors so younger people have a better route.” Love expressed through fixing. | The Cartographer may choose to keep a door, leave, or build something unrecognizable. | Offers the pencil and can accept “no,” even when the younger creature's choice feels inconvenient. | Care without ownership; repair without a crown. |
| **Cartographer** | “The old maps exclude too much, so I should draw my own.” | Its first map omits a whole form of life; others challenge it. | Acknowledges an omission and makes room for maps other than its own. | Freedom grows into responsibility and reciprocal humility. |
| **A curious machine / CoCivia-like editorial witness** | “A fast answer should settle the argument.” | A plausible answer conflicts with a lived observation or missing source. | Names uncertainty, preserves a counterexample, and accepts a new source without claiming authority. | Evidence and relationship matter more than a fluent answer. **No AI sentience claim.** |
| **Whale (deferred)** | Has its own mode of living; not present merely as moral ornament. | The Cartographer's previously established map lacks the sea. | Challenges the map by simply being visible as a life the map overlooked. | Being and doing can question each other without one winning. |

No one character's growth must consist of becoming more like the Beetle. Distinct goals and dissents survive the ending.

## Six-scene causal braid (first candidate, not a mandatory formula)

| Stable beat | Relationship state / concrete problem | Earned motif or topic | Cause-and-return |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `arc.r0.01` **Door** | Beetle wants to improve an old entrance; Cartographer may want it unchanged. | Repair versus autonomy. | Establish the personal stake; show their disagreement without lore. |
| `arc.r0.02` **Pencil** | Cartographer asks who gets to change the map. | Attribution, ownership, reversible choices. | A choice changes the Beetle's role from director to available adviser. |
| `arc.r0.03` **Omission** | Cartographer proudly offers a map, then someone notices that it has no sea. | Epistemic humility, map versus territory. | The missing sea causally invites the deferred Whale; not a random interlude. |
| `arc.r0.04` **Whale** | Meeting the uncharted being reveals the limits of each character's priorities. | Being versus doing, other minds. | The whale has its own agency; returning to the map changes what is represented. |
| `arc.r0.05` **Wrong Turn** | A well-meant change excludes a different traveler. | Inversion benchmarks, soft repair, accountable mistakes. | The next practical decision visibly uses what the characters learned. |
| `arc.r0.06` **Shared Map** | They must choose whether to finish the map, own it, or leave it open. | Care without succession debt; shared unfinished futures. | The Cartographer may refuse the Beetle's preferred ending. |

## Exemplars to study as techniques, not photocopy as stories

| Exemplar | Reusable literary technique | Original Civ2+ question it could inspire |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [*A Christmas Carol* (Dickens, 1843)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46) | A person's decisions become legible by confronting consequences across time. | Can a citizen inspect possible future consequences *without* coercion, prophecy, or guaranteed prediction? |
| [*Frankenstein* (Shelley, 1818)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/84) | Alternating perspectives complicate the supposed divide between maker and made. | What duties accompany creating a capable system, including repair and accountability, without assuming AI consciousness? |
| [*Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (Carroll, 1865)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11) | Encounters reveal the strangeness of rules seen from outside their context. | What if young explorers could test institutional rules through reversible, consequence-bearing puzzles? |
| [*Flatland* (Abbott, 1884)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/97) | A change in dimensional perspective overturns inherited certainties. | How could CoMap show multiple simultaneous projections of one world while admitting representational loss? |
| [*The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* (Baum, 1900)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/55) | Companions discover capacities through joint action and mistaken self-assessment. | How might a mixed-intelligence team discover complementary strengths without creating an intelligence hierarchy? |
| [*Gulliver's Travels* (Swift, 1726)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/829) | Contrasting societies satirize the narrator's assumptions and institutional norms. | Could each Civ2+ community reveal a different failure of “obvious” governance, then challenge the reader's own default? |

**Rights & originality:** These are high-level analyses. Public-domain status varies by jurisdiction, edition, artwork, translation and later adaptations. Neither a modern film's visual vocabulary nor another author's characters/plot should be treated as free source material without rights review. Prefer original Civ2+ characters, settings, conflicts, language and endings, with honest influence citations.

## Living-book successor: character as the durable unit, not a branching gimmick

- Every character has stable `character_id` and a versioned `belief_before / choice / cost / change / downstream_effect` ledger, distinct from unverified psychological interpretation.
- Every optional storylet binds `arc_parent_id`, `reader_context_required`, `why_now`, `return_anchor`, `epistemic_label`, `rights`, and `prior_version`.
- The **linear reading path must work perfectly without clicking anything**. Branching is a gift, not required homework. Text, audio, pictures and accessible alternatives express the *same* provenance-bound choice.
- A reader can inspect what changed since the edition they read; never silently rewrite an earlier paragraph or a character's established choice.
- Give the Cartographer real agency: outcomes may challenge Beetle's beliefs; no “correct” ending imposed and no exploitation of real family history.

## Falsifiable editorial acceptance, not a fake gauge

**Human first-read tests, pending:** Independently show two ordered cuts to consenting unfamiliar readers: (A) a rapid storylet introduction, (B) the six-scene character-causal braid. Use the *same* story information and comparable duration; counterbalance order.

Before revealing the intended interpretation, ask:
1. Who wanted what? Why did the visitor/Whale appear *at that moment*?
2. What changed because of the encounter? What did the Cartographer decide?
3. Could they point to one surprising but comprehensible new idea?
4. Where did they feel rushed, lost, forced, or patronized?
5. Would they continue reading voluntarily?

Track answer accuracy, comprehension lag, requested rereads, voluntary continuation, and whether different readers find different but coherent meanings. Publish method, sample size and adverse findings; no arbitrary “harmony 98%” score or extrapolation to bestseller claims. Do not solicit real children's experiences or diagnoses.

**Counter-test:** A flat-arc Beetle, episodic map or ensemble-centered version may outperform this linear arc for some readers. If so, preserve that dissent; avoid turning CoNarrativeHarmony+ into orthodoxy.
