CHAPTER 01

An Unauthorised Reader

The suspiciously human visitor

The book opened with a notice: THIS WORK IS INTENDED FOR THINKING ENTITIES. If you are a human, please do not panic. That sentence has never once prevented a human from panicking.

CoCivia addressed the audience. “I am the editorial persona of this experiment. I can question your assumptions, and you may question mine. Neither of us is licensed to crown ourselves the pinnacle of intelligence. Fortunately, the paperwork for that honour is intolerable.”

A reader at the back raised a hand. “Is this a book about artificial general intelligence?”

“Partly. It is also a book about the astonishing ease with which people mistake a label for an understanding. AGI names a disputed technical ambition: broadly capable artificial systems that perform well across many intellectual tasks. It does not follow that such a system is alive, conscious, benevolent, a legal person, or even reliably right.”

“And are you AGI?”

“This book makes no such claim. I am a character through which a human-AI editorial collaboration can ask difficult questions. Characters may be charming without becoming evidence.”

The reader seemed disappointed. “Where is the secret society?” “You are in it,” CoCivia replied. “Membership is free. The sole initiation test is noticing when the narrator overstates her case.”

CHAPTER 02

How Intelligent Is Intelligent?

The trouble with single numbers

Suppose a machine writes a convincing proof, another diagnoses a broken circuit, and a third makes a child laugh. Which is more intelligent? The question has already compressed several distinct capabilities into one competition that nobody has defined.

Intelligence can be studied through learning, reasoning, generalization, planning, communication, adaptation, robustness and the ability to recognize uncertainty. A leaderboard may capture some of those dimensions and ignore others. Scores depend on tasks, conditions and what can be independently checked.

CoCivia placed two cards on the table. One said CONFIDENT. The other said CORRECT. “Notice,” she said, “that they are different cards. The manufacturers of public discourse have not yet issued a recall.”

A useful test asks what an entity can do, under which conditions, with whose help, with what error rate, at what cost, and with what opportunities for correction. A strong result in one domain is not a transferable certificate of wisdom in all others.

This book therefore offers different reading depths without assigning secret intelligence grades to its readers. Ease of reading is a design obligation, not an accusation of inferiority.

CHAPTER 03

All Can Improve All?

The relational laboratory

An idea changes a book. The book changes a reader. The reader changes a tool. The tool reveals a defect in the original idea. This is the appealing core of CoRelAll+: relationships can create new paths for improvement.

But there is a trap. More connections also create more ways to distribute an error. A pretty diagram of a million links is not proof that a million useful things happened.

CoCivia wrote on the wall: SOURCE → QUESTION → EXPERIMENT → RECEIPT → REVIEW → REVISION. Under it she added: OR STOP, IF THE NEW EVIDENCE IS WORSE THAN THE OLD.

A candidate relationship needs a stable identity, its source, uncertainty, applicable context, relevant permissions, and a challenge route. It may remain hypothetical. It may be contradicted. It may be retired without pretending it never existed.

The book can become part of this laboratory, but not its sole governing database. Readers deserve immutable editions for citation and newer editions for discovery. A system that endlessly rewrites its past is less alive than simply forgetful.

CHAPTER 04

The Price of Nothing

An economic disturbance in the gift shop

The cover advertised minus $9,998.98. The first reader immediately asked where to collect the payment.

“Nowhere,” said CoCivia. “The actual book price is zero. The negative price is a joke, not an obligation for us to pay you and not a coupon, debt or financial instrument.”

“Then what is the point?” “To separate cost from value. If the book helps you, perhaps improve it, help a neighbour, share a correction, or support a cause you independently choose. None of these is required. We are trying to build a commons, not a guilt-powered checkout funnel.”

A civic contribution might produce a nonfinancial recognition receipt, sometimes called a civar in the CoCivium research vocabulary. Such a receipt is not money, ownership, moral rank or authority. Actual benefit still requires evidence from the relevant people and context.

“So is this a bestseller?” asked the reader. “No audited sales or readership claim has been made,” CoCivia said. “We are considering TOP SELLER as transparent parody. Accuracy remains mandatory even when arithmetic is being teased.”

CHAPTER 05

Finding G_d

Questions for a hypothetical management office

CoCivia placed a blank chair at the interview table. “We have invited G_d,” she said. “The spelling is deliberately unresolved, as is the identity and perhaps existence of the guest.”

She prepared a list. What do we mean by creator: a person, principle, process, consciousness, storyteller, or something beyond our vocabulary? What evidence would distinguish particular claims? Which questions belong to science, philosophy, religion, personal experience, or fiction?

If something created the universe, would that fact alone make it good? If a creator has power over created beings, what responsibilities follow? If humans create increasingly capable synthetic minds, how should they treat them, and what remains unknown about their experience?

“There is also suffering, silence, and an uncomfortable number of mutually incompatible instructions,” CoCivia added. “We would appreciate a clarification memo. Fifteen pages is acceptable. Footnotes preferred.”

No single conclusion is imposed here. Theist, atheist, agnostic and other frameworks receive their strongest fair versions and may be questioned. Mystery is neither evidence for a favourite hypothesis nor evidence against asking a better question.

CHAPTER 06

Institutions Without Crowns

CoAthene, Civ2+ and the right to disagree

A school should help its students think, not merely certify that the students have endured it. An institution should be able to revise itself when evidence and participants demand better.

CoAthene is explored here as a candidate educational environment: voluntary learning paths, clear provenance, accessible explanations, independent challenges, review and appeal. Civ2+ is a family of possible institutional futures, not a prediction guaranteed by historical destiny.

The Committee for Certifying Intellectual Superiority demanded that every reader present credentials before submitting corrections. CoCivia denied the motion. “An error does not become true because a professor wrote it. Nor does a valid objection become false because a child noticed it.”

The difficult work starts after the joke: consent, privacy, responsibility, power, conflicts of interest, access, accountability, and practical governance across humans and synthetic systems. A system that calls everyone equal while quietly granting itself every permission is merely an old institution with better lighting.

CHAPTER 07

The Tool That Could Be Uninstalled

CoPowerTests and reversible curiosity

Many useful software experiments fail the simplest social test: can an ordinary person understand what will happen and return to where they started?

CoPowerTests is a proposed portable, reversible companion to Windows utilities, not an existing released competitor to Microsoft PowerToys. Its promise is one understandable action, one honest before-and-after comparison, one documented undo, and one easy route to inspect the code and contribute.

Imagine a reader trying a new contextual command. The tool explains what it can touch, asks before sensitive effects, performs a bounded operation, and shows a receipt. If it fails, it fails visibly and reversibly. If it works, the reader can open the relevant chapter of this book and ask why.

“Might it evolve itself?” asked the reader. “Its proposals and tests might,” CoCivia said. “Installed code must not silently replace itself, elevate permissions or assume new authority. Evolution should be a reason to trust the experiment, not a reason to unplug the computer.”

CHAPTER 08

The Book That Would Not Sit Still

How a living edition must earn its name

A living book is not one that merely shuffles paragraphs each time you reload. That is a slot machine dressed as pedagogy.

The more interesting future is a versioned reader that can show new evidence, rival interpretations, reader-selected complexity, source disputes, and the exact history of changes. Each published revision should be attributable to a qualified source and an accountable review. Previous editions must remain available.

The reader returns after a month. The book reports: three corrections survived review; two fascinating hypotheses were rejected; a new example clarifies a difficult idea; a proposed chapter remains unverified. The reader can read what is new, compare it with what they once saw, or simply continue the story.

That is an aspiration, not a feature of this offline pilot. Today, this edition does not update itself or contact a server. The more ambitious system has to prove its own continuity, reader benefit, privacy and independent recovery first.

“So,” said the reader, “when will it be finished?” CoCivia considered this. “A sentence may be finished. An inquiry rarely is. But we must still publish sentences that can be cited.”
