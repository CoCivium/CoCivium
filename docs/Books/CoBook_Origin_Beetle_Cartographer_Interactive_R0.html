The Beetle and the Unfinished Map

A little creature, a younger cartographer, and a city certain that every door should open the same way.

One scene. Three valid routes. You can leave the map unfinished.

A city of doors

In a city full of doors, nearly every door came with instructions. Some kept everyone safe. Others had survived so long that nobody remembered what they were for.

The Beetle had spent years inspecting doors. Sometimes it had been right. Sometimes it had been insufferable. On its better days, it knew the difference.

Then the Cartographer came along, asking questions the Beetle hadn't considered.

Cartographer Why do you keep working on doors you don't want to walk through?

Beetle I have a rather selfish reason. I want you to inherit more choices than I did.

Cartographer What if I like one of those doors?

Beetle Then keep it.

Keep the door

Keep the door: The Cartographer keeps the useful doorway and changes its instructions. The Beetle learns that preserving something can be as thoughtful as replacing it.

Draw another: The Cartographer draws an opening nobody had pictured. The Beetle offers a pencil, then stops explaining where the line should go.

Walk elsewhere: They leave the door behind and discover a field without walls. The map is not a demand to finish every project begun by a parent.

One kind of inheritance: A map of what someone learned, not a debt to follow their route. We can be inversion benchmarks for one another without becoming one another's stepping stones.
