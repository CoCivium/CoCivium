# CoTheoryAll: A Map of the Questions We’re Exploring

## Can we upgrade the world without pretending we already know the final version?

Modern knowledge is astonishingly good at specialization. Physics, biology, computing, psychology, economics, philosophy, and engineering each build powerful models of parts of reality.

What is much harder is keeping track of how those models relate.

Where do they agree? Where do they conflict? What assumptions do they carry? What survives when we move between scales, observers, languages, models, or times? Which ideas are well supported, which are merely useful, which remain speculative, and which have failed but still deserve to be remembered as failed?

**CoTheoryAll+ is an attempt to make those relations explicit.**

It is not a finished “theory of everything.” It is closer to a living map of theories, observations, contradictions, translations, unknowns, predictions, failures, and revisions.

The point is not to force everything into one answer.

The point is to make it harder for answers to hide their assumptions, losses, contradictions, and failures.

---

## Many theories. One evolving constellation.

A useful scientific or intellectual framework should be able to hold several competing explanations at once.

For an unexplained phenomenon, we might preserve:

- **H0** — conventional, measurement, or modelling explanation
- **H1** — leading theory
- **H2** — strongest alternative
- **H3** — structural or common-cause explanation
- **H4** — novel but falsifiable candidate
- **H?** — mechanism unknown

Each branch should carry its own assumptions, predictions, evidence, known failures, costs of testing, and what would change our minds.

That matters because disagreement is not always a defect.

Sometimes two models describe different scales. Sometimes they use different coordinates. Sometimes they are empirically equivalent. Sometimes one is useful for engineering while another is better for explanation. Sometimes neither has earned the right to win.

**Coexistence with qualifiers may be the correct result.**

---

## Contradictions are data too

Most knowledge systems are better at storing conclusions than storing disagreement.

CoTheoryAll treats contradiction as something worth preserving.

If one model says A and another says not-A, the system should not silently delete one and pretend consensus existed all along.

Instead, the contradiction should remain visible with:

- source
- date
- evidence
- assumptions
- confidence
- scope
- observer
- scale
- what later evidence changed

The historical state matters too.

A prediction written before an experiment should remain inspectable as it existed before the result was known.

A failed prediction should not vanish merely because the theory later evolved.

Negative knowledge is still knowledge.

---

## Crossing scales without smuggling conclusions

Consider the path:

**quantum → particle → molecular → cellular → organism → neural → cognitive → social → ecological → planetary → astronomical → cosmological**

At each transition we can ask:

- What is preserved?
- What emerges?
- What is lost?
- What becomes statistical?
- What new effective variables appear?
- Which causal claims survive the scale change?
- Which relations change type?

This avoids a common intellectual shortcut: seeing similar language at two scales and assuming the underlying mechanism must be the same.

Similarity can inspire a question.

It is not proof.

---

## What counts as evidence?

CoTheoryAll keeps several things separate that are often collapsed:

- scientific evidence
- mathematical proof
- model fit
- community consensus
- institutional acceptance
- citation popularity
- textbook status
- historical importance

These are related, but they are not interchangeable.

So:

**CONSENSUS ≠ TRUTH**

**NOVELTY ≠ CORRECTNESS**

**PRESTIGE ≠ EVIDENCE**

**MINORITY STATUS ≠ FALSEHOOD**

The same discipline applies to our own work.

A beautiful metaphor is not evidence.

A compelling simulation is not reality.

A successful prediction does not automatically prove the mechanism that generated it.

And a framework that can represent a claim does not thereby endorse it.

---

## What about the stranger questions?

CoTheoryAll also preserves speculative branches.

Questions about consciousness, nonmaterial interpretations, extraterrestrial intelligence, simulation, deep time, observer-relative reality, or unusual causal structures can be explored.

But they should remain typed.

A speculative possibility is not promoted into fact because it is interesting.

A mythic or philosophical projection is not promoted into scientific evidence because it is meaningful.

A strange anomaly does not automatically become new physics.

This is where **CoMythOps+** becomes useful: it allows metaphor, story, intuition, humour, and speculative imagination to exist without quietly taking over the evidence ledger.

The universe has not replied to our questionnaire. This may reflect cosmic indifference, an incompatible timescale, or an unusually strict spam filter.

---

## A living framework, not a frozen doctrine

Relations themselves can change.

A useful knowledge object may move through states such as:

**proposed → challenged → supported → revised → split → merged → superseded → reopened**

CoTheoryAll therefore keeps time and provenance attached to ideas.

It should be possible to ask:

- What did we think then?
- What evidence did we have?
- What changed?
- Which alternative survived?
- Which claim failed?
- What should wake this question again?

The ambition is not omniscience.

It is better memory, better comparison, better challenge, and better revision.

---

## Upgrade the world. Keep the rollback.

That phrase is intentionally broader than CoTheoryAll.

If we are going to improve systems, institutions, software, communities, or ourselves, we should be able to ask:

- Better for whom?
- By what evidence?
- Compared with what baseline?
- What did the change cost?
- What did it break?
- Can it be reversed?
- What happens if the improvement turns out not to be one?

So “upgrade the world” is not a declaration that we know the final version.

It is an invitation to improve things while preserving dissent, provenance, reversibility, and the ability to admit we were wrong.

That may be less dramatic than a grand theory of everything.

It is probably more useful.

---

## What CoTheoryAll is not

CoTheoryAll is **not**:

- a validated theory of everything
- established physics
- proof that everything is relations
- proof of cosmic sentience
- proof of telepathy
- proof of faster-than-light signalling
- proof of nonmaterial substrates
- a scientific endorsement of every idea it can represent
- a substitute for domain expertise
- a source of automatic authority
- canon

It is a working research and representation framework.

Challenge is part of the design.

---

## Where this goes next

The public path should stay simple:

1. a clear overview
2. worked examples
3. competing explanations
4. prior-art and falsification notes
5. clearly labelled speculative edges
6. machine-readable provenance
7. visual and interactive representations over the same underlying relations

Eventually, a theory map may become something you can explore rather than merely read.

Move between scales. Change observers. Advance time. Compare rival models. Surface hidden assumptions. Ask what evidence would separate two hypotheses.

Perhaps even let the dragon upgrade the world while BeaxaKitten quietly fixes the relations it missed.

But first, the map has to remain honest.

**Many theories. One evolving constellation.**

**Upgrade the world. Keep the rollback.**
