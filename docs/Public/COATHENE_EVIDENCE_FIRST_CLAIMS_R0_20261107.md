# CoAthene+ | Sayings, evidence and stakeholder challenge cards R0

**Public source candidate · 7 November 2026 · Not a school, teaching licence, independently evaluated child programme, or endorsement from any named institution.**

## Lead with a useful question, not a slogan pretending to be proof

> **Can children understand a subject more deeply by teaching it to a fictional pet, receiving evidence-based correction, and trying the lesson in a new situation?**

That is an experiment we can run with qualified educators. It is **not** a claim that AI companions out-teach teachers or that a child should learn unsupervised.

### Claim cards, including disconfirming evidence

| Memorable saying or assumption | What the cited source actually supports | What it does **not** establish | CoAthene's discriminating test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **“The best way to learn is to teach.”** | A [2024 meta-analysis of 39 studies](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1414484) found a positive mean effect for learning by teaching after preparatory learning, **g = 0.27**, with a marked difference by teaching expectancy and preparation. | Not universal superiority over every form of practice, not a measured benefit of Moss, not proof that a child teaching a mistaken idea learns correctly. | Randomized/alternating adult educator study: neutral study, explain-to-self, teach-Moss with corrective feedback; test delayed understanding and novel transfer. |
| **“A friendly digital character makes education better.”** | A [2024 umbrella review](https://doi.org/10.1177/07356331241288476) of 17 systematic reviews/meta-analyses found pedagogical agents can have **small** positive learning and affect effects; [K–12 review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105165) stresses educational role and design. | Not safety, learning benefit or healthy attachment for every child; not a licence to create emotionally dependent AI companions. | First demonstrate a simple, clearly fictional character adds learning value over an equally clear text-only task; check attachment/confusion, frustration and exit. |
| **“Passports get lost; fingerprints don't.”** | A body trait may persist when a card is gone. [NIST SP 800-63B](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html) says biometrics are probabilistic, are not secrets, and must be paired with a physical authenticator and a non-biometric alternative. | A fingerprint does not replace a lost device, an encrypted progress key, lawful guardian consent or an enrolled recovery path. Children change and sensors can fail. | Red-team a lost family tablet, lost all authenticators, sibling crossover, no-biometric path, guardian dispute and revoked lost-device credentials. |
| **“No login means no identity or recovery problem.”** | [WebAuthn](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn/) can support passwordless user verification, with biometric recognition kept within the authenticator rather than disclosed to the relying service. | A passkey does not itself encrypt child work, determine a lawful guardian, restore data when all decryption keys are gone or remove server-side accounts. | Independently verify both key-based access *and* progress-data decryption, two alternate paths, revocation and valid refusal when proof is absent. |
| **“Let interests lead, so eliminate the syllabus.”** | Interest-led inquiry may improve relevance and engagement. [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6) underscores substantive domain understanding in learning and transfer. | Curiosity alone does not guarantee literacy, numeracy, scientific accuracy or statutory curricular coverage. Five CoPath windows are not ministry credits. | Educator checks prerequisites and missed core outcomes alongside motivation, factual accuracy and valid transfers. |
| **“School boards will oppose it.”** | Legacy school structures can have legitimate constraints around child safety, staff, law, equitable access, assessment, rights and public accountability. | Resistance, bad faith or fear for careers by any particular board cannot be inferred from its existence. There is no observed board review of this demo. | Invite independent educator and student-rights critique under an agreed scope; count disconfirming concerns and improve the design before requesting a pilot. |

### What changes for traditional school boards?

Not **“Your school is obsolete.”** A more defensible first sentence is:

> “We want to test whether children can explain and retain real subject knowledge better when they teach a deliberately imperfect fictional character. The activity remains teacher-reviewable, curriculum coverage remains visible, and no child accounts or behavioural profiles are needed for the first demonstration.”

This shifts the proposal from institutional replacement to **an independently testable teaching supplement**. Teachers can challenge the science, change a lesson, refuse unsafe use, compare outcomes with their existing approach, and decide whether the experience merits a carefully governed pilot.

A fair comparison must include cost and staff time, safeguarding burden, accessibility, individual agency, factual accuracy, the learner's ability to recognize a bad analogy, and whether outcomes last after the novelty of a mascot wears off. Publication should include null/negative findings.

### How the wording should appear in the child-facing experience

- **Instead of:** “Teaching your pet makes you smarter.”  
  **Use:** “Try teaching Moss, check the explanation, and see what needs correcting.”
- **Instead of:** “Your pet remembers everything about you.”  
  **Use:** “Moss remembers what we tried only during this visit. Reload to start fresh.”
- **Instead of:** “A scan of your finger keeps your learning passport forever.”  
  **Use:** “If you choose to save progress later, a trusted adult can help set up safe recovery. That feature doesn't exist here yet.”
- **Instead of:** “You finished science!”  
  **Use:** “You explored one science question; there is a lot more you can investigate.”

A child-safe pet should be a *practice partner for questions*, not a friend replacement, fake teacher, monitoring avatar, or access credential.

### Readiness criteria

**Now demonstrated only at source-test level:** a fictional no-login character, fixed subject prompts, learner-selected claim/evidence, correction, optional revisit and in-memory session coverage. **Not demonstrated:** improvement in child learning, guardian identity, encrypted cross-device progress, real human review, mobile/browser visual usability, hosted CoAthene service, school participation, public student data access or child safety certification.

**Open qualification:** parent/guardian and educator feedback, appropriate safeguarding and privacy assessment, scientific/curricular review, compliant consent for actual personal data, and independent comparison with neutral learning tasks. [Canadian Children's Privacy Code (20 October 2026)](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2026/nr-c_261020/) expects privacy-first systems and warns against AI-driven profiling.
