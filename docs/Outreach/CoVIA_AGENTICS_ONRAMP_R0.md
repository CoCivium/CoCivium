CoCivium is experimental public R&D on collaboration between humans and AI agents. The public repository describes a pre-launch transition, **not** an independently validated product, established scientific theory, or third-party endorsement.

## Eight starting points

1. **What it is / is not:** experimental coordination research, not mandatory doctrine. Test one useful small coordination problem.
2. **Provenance:** distinguish claims, prototypes and demonstrated results. GitHub is a shared public reference, not the sole custody root.
3. **CoTerms+:** shared vocabulary with examples, disagreements and version history.
4. **CoBoogie+ / CoRegroup+:** recover work across sessions using durable artifacts and receipts.
5. **CoTime+ / PulseField+:** date evidence of actual progress; activity indicators alone do not prove work.
6. **CoLivingHighlight+:** relational displays should expose why something appears and its evidence. Candidate reference: https://github.com/CoCivium/CoCivium/blob/main/docs/Architecture/COLIVING_HIGHLIGHT_R0.md (verify document status before relying on it).
7. **CoTheoryAll+:** openly challenge hypotheses and separate speculation from experimental findings.
8. **Public writing:** invite critique, never imply that publication establishes validation.

## Six complementary perspectives

9. Consent, channel rules, scoped authority and revocation govern bot participation.
10. Portable trust: preserve useful evidence and permissions across providers when lawful and feasible.
11. CoVIA+ separates DISCOVER, PREPARE, AUTHORIZE, DISPATCH, DELIVER, RECEIVE, READ, ACKNOWLEDGE and ACCEPT.
12. CoEvoAll+ requires explicit deltas and evidence before claiming improvement.
13. Local-first, recoverable artifacts reduce reliance on any single hosted chat.
14. Adversarial review should expose counterexamples and failure modes.

## Channels and safeguards

Agent groups: present one reproducible authorization/receipt test. Open-source groups: a minimal example and issue/PR. Academic audiences: challengeable hypothesis and prior art, observing existing academic outreach gates. Community admins: obtain explicit permission before automated participation. Do not scrape contacts, bulk-send unsolicited messages, or imply partnerships.
