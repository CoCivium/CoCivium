# CoAthene+ RoamLab R0 — Full Business Plan

**Status:** public StrawBe+ candidate  
**Pilot geography:** Oakville, Ontario, Canada  
**Operating posture:** useful standalone; optional CoAll relations  
**Important:** illustrative business planning only. Operators must obtain their own legal, tax, insurance, educational, child-safety, accessibility and transportation advice.

## 1. Problem

Schools, families and enrichment operators face several recurring constraints:

- field trips require transportation, approvals, supervision and time;
- immersive technology is expensive and operationally awkward for individual classrooms;
- many VR products are novelty experiences with weak curriculum/evidence links;
- local attractions and businesses often have expertise but limited ability to reach classrooms;
- after-school programs can become repetitive or screen-centric;
- smaller schools and groups may not justify permanent immersive facilities.

RoamLab proposes to bring a reusable immersive learning substrate to the learner, connect it to real local places, and preserve a clear distinction between simulation and evidence.

## 2. Solution

A RoamLab session combines some or all of:

- VR/MR/AR;
- large-screen shared visualization;
- local AI assistance;
- physical experiments;
- environmental/science sensing;
- maps and primary sources;
- maker/robotics tasks;
- expert participation;
- a real destination before or after the immersive session.

The educational structure is:

1. ask;
2. model;
3. predict;
4. encounter evidence;
5. compare;
6. revise;
7. explain/create;
8. continue.

This makes the immersive layer part of an inquiry loop instead of a headset amusement queue.

## 3. Initial product

### Portable RoamLab

Start with a portable system before buying a dedicated vehicle.

Candidate equipment:

- 6-12 standalone immersive headsets;
- tablets;
- facilitator laptop;
- local Wi-Fi/router;
- local content cache;
- display/projector;
- charging and secure transport cases;
- replaceable/washable facial interfaces;
- basic sensors;
- maker materials;
- accessibility alternatives.

This version can be delivered in an ordinary classroom, library, rented room, community centre or sponsor site.

### Parked mobile lab

If portable pilots justify it, move to a van, trailer, shuttle or bus outfitted as a stationary lab.

The lab travels with staff and equipment, parks, and then receives learners while stationary.

Possible features:

- 8-20 learner stations;
- modular seating/tables;
- large display;
- local compute/network;
- non-headset learning station;
- secure charging;
- ventilation/environment sensors;
- maker/science bench;
- exterior awning/pop-up zone;
- accessible station where vehicle geometry permits.

### Passenger transport

Do not make passenger transport part of v1.

A later passenger variant must be separately qualified for vehicle, licensing, CVOR, school-purpose rules, inspections, insurance, accessibility and board/vendor requirements.

## 5. Oakville pilot logic

Current public evidence supports Oakville as a reasonable pilot location:

- more than 10,000 businesses with employees;
- strong advanced manufacturing, ICT/digital media, health/life sciences and professional-services sectors;
- curriculum-linked Town and Oakville Museum school programs;
- current Oakville Transit charter pilot for school groups attending Town Recreation and Culture programs;
- Conservation Halton curriculum-linked education at Crawford Lake and Mountsberg;
- Sheridan Trafalgar Campus and immersive/experiential design capacity;
- Oakville Chamber network;
- Town sponsorship and advertising channels.

These are not endorsements. They are an unusually dense local relation field for testing.

## 6. First seven modules

### Oakville Then / Oakville Now

Learners compare historical maps, buildings, primary sources and virtual reconstruction, then visit or study Oakville Museum/Old Oakville evidence.

### Watershed Explorer

Learners model rainfall, runoff, habitat and biodiversity, then compare against local outdoor observations.

### Build the Better City

Learners experiment with transport, land use, housing, energy, parks and accessibility in a simulation, then compare trade-offs against the real town.

### Inside the Machine

Forces, gears, motors, robots, manufacturing and maintenance. Local manufacturing/engineering experts can provide bounded real-world context.

### Human Body / Evidence Lab

Anatomy, imaging, systems, uncertainty and health-data privacy. Not diagnosis.

### Immersive Story Studio

Storyboarding, spatial narrative, animation, game mechanics, accessibility and critique.

### Space / Earth / Climate

Scale, orbital systems, atmosphere, remote sensing and the difference between observation and model.

## 9. Unit economics

Track contribution margin rather than gross revenue.

Core equation:

required school bookings = (monthly fixed costs - sponsor contribution - after-school/community contribution) / contribution margin per school booking

Illustrative only:

- monthly fixed cost: 22,500
- sponsor contribution: 5,000
- after-school/community contribution: 7,000
- contribution margin per school booking: 550

This yields about 20 school-booking equivalents per month.

Track:

- contribution per operating hour;
- repeat booking;
- sponsor renewal;
- setup/reset time;
- facilitator utilization;
- equipment damage/loss;
- learner throughput;
- acquisition cost;
- cash runway;
- gross margin by module.

## 12. Safety, privacy and accessibility

Default privacy:

- no facial recognition;
- no emotion inference;
- no advertising profile;
- no sale of learner data;
- no persistent biometric profile;
- no unnecessary recording;
- no hidden recording;
- minimum-data attendance;
- local/offline option;
- deletion/export process;
- school/guardian consent where required.

Headset/physical safety:

- manufacturer age/use requirements;
- clear boundaries;
- seated option;
- motion-sickness stop rule;
- sanitation;
- fit/comfort checks;
- active facilitator observation;
- breaks;
- clear fire/egress path.

Accessibility:

- wheelchair-accessible path where feasible;
- seated mode;
- non-headset equivalent;
- captions;
- motion-reduced content;
- high-contrast/large-text modes;
- controller alternatives;
- sensory-sensitive mode;
- quiet option;
- colour-independent cues.

## 17. Investment and pay-it-forward

The free plan creates no investment right.

Anyone can use/fork the plan without offering equity.

Possible capital forms for an independent operator include:

- founder capital;
- grant;
- donation;
- sponsorship;
- equipment-in-kind;
- debt;
- revenue-based financing;
- recoverable grant;
- minority equity;
- co-operative/community capital where lawful.

No instrument is universally preferred.

If Rick/CoCivium later considers investing, that should be a separate diligence event with its own valuation, legal/tax/securities review, conflict disclosure, downside analysis and governance boundaries.

No investment should buy:

- learner data;
- curriculum control;
- preferential CoAll authority;
- exclusivity over the open plan;
- suppression of competing forks.

## 18. Success metrics

Educational:

- learner can explain a model revision;
- teacher reports real curriculum value;
- immersive layer adds value beyond cheaper media;
- accessibility path works;
- simulation/reality boundary stays clear.

Commercial:

- paid repeat booking;
- sponsor renewal;
- positive contribution margin;
- stable staff load;
- manageable device failure;
- cash runway.

Public-good:

- sponsored/free seats;
- open lesson reuse;
- independent operators;
- accessibility improvements;
- published corrections;
- non-CoAll benefit.

## 19. Kill/redesign conditions

Shrink or stop if:

- people enjoy the demo but do not pay;
- novelty explains most of the effect;
- setup consumes too much class time;
- accessibility remains poor;
- persistent profiling becomes necessary;
- sponsors demand control;
- portable delivery beats bus economics;
- insurance/regulation overwhelms value;
- cheaper teaching tools perform as well or better.
