# Sources — Watershed Explorer R0

**Checked:** 2026-09-30

These sources ground the local example. They do not imply endorsement or partnership.

## Town of Oakville — Stormwater Management

https://www.oakville.ca/home-environment/stormwater/stormwater-management/

Current Town material describes stormwater as precipitation/runoff moving from properties and hard surfaces through drainage systems toward local water bodies.

The page currently reports infrastructure including:

- 622 km of storm pipes;
- 30,200 catch basins;
- 67 stormwater-management ponds;
- 16 creeks totalling 156 km;
- 8.07 km of shoreline;
- 168 km of ditches.

Use these as dated local-context facts, not permanent constants.

## Town of Oakville — Storm Sewer Network

https://www.oakville.ca/home-environment/stormwater/storm-sewer-network/

Current Town material describes the storm-sewer network as underground pipes, ditches, channels, catch basins, culverts and related structures that move runoff toward Lake Ontario, creeks or stormwater-management ponds.

## Town of Oakville — Stormwater Management Ponds

https://www.oakville.ca/home-environment/stormwater/stormwater-management-ponds/

Current Town material explains that developed hard surfaces reduce infiltration and can increase rapid runoff, and that stormwater-management ponds help manage runoff quantity and quality before water enters creeks/Lake Ontario.

## Town of Oakville — Creeks

https://www.oakville.ca/home-environment/stormwater/creeks/

Current Town material identifies Sixteen Mile Creek and Bronte Creek as major watercourses plus 14 other named creeks, and describes periodic creek-condition assessment and resident practices relevant to erosion/water quality.

## Town of Oakville — Rainwater Management Plan

https://www.oakville.ca/home-environment/stormwater/rainwater-management-plan/

Current Town material describes stormwater infrastructure and natural assets including storm sewers, catch basins, ditches, roads/overland flow, creeks, ponds and shoreline embankments.

## Conservation Halton — Education

https://www.conservationhalton.ca/education/

Current page lists a Grade 8 program called **Blue Planet** at Kelso, available seasonally, covering water quality, human impacts, watershed/water-flow concepts and field activities such as a stream study and water-quality tests.

Watershed Explorer does not reproduce or represent that program. It treats it as a possible field-extension relation.

## Ontario STEM / Science curriculum context

https://news.ontario.ca/en/backgrounder/1001723/supporting-stem-achievement-in-ontario

Ontario's revised Grades 1-8 Science and Technology curriculum introduced in 2022 emphasizes hands-on research, engineering design, environmental protection, emerging technologies and experiential learning.

Watershed Explorer is a **candidate curriculum-support module**, not a Ministry-approved curriculum resource. Teachers/schools should map the exact current expectations appropriate to their grade/course.
