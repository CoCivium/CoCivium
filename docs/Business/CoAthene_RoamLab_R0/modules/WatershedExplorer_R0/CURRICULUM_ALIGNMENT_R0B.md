## Purpose

This file maps Watershed Explorer R0A to current Ontario Grade 8 Science and Technology expectations where there is a plausible instructional relation.

It does **not** claim:

- Ministry approval;
- school-board approval;
- curriculum completeness;
- that using the lesson automatically satisfies any expectation;
- that assessment/evaluation decisions belong to CoCivium rather than the educator.

## Strong candidate alignments

### A1 — STEM Investigation and Communication Skills

Current Grade 8 expectations include using scientific research, experimentation and engineering-design processes, following appropriate health/safety procedures, and communicating findings.

Watershed Explorer candidate relations:

- precommitted prediction;
- controlled model test;
- simple measurement controls;
- observation recording;
- source challenge;
- revised explanation;
- named uncertainty;
- proposed next measurement;
- explicit safety stop/fallbacks.

**Strength:** STRONG_CANDIDATE_ALIGNMENT

**Gap:** the module currently provides one bounded investigation rather than a full progression across the strand.

### E1 — Relating Science and Technology to Our Changing World / Water Systems

Current Grade 8 Water Systems expectations include assessing impacts of human activities/technologies on water resources and considering sustainability.

Watershed Explorer candidate relations:

- hard/permeable surface comparison;
- municipal stormwater infrastructure;
- runoff quantity/quality;
- local human-built drainage relations;
- optional destination/field extension;
- source challenge requiring learners to limit claims.

**Strength:** MODERATE_TO_STRONG_CANDIDATE_ALIGNMENT

**Gap:** R0A does not yet require a full social/environmental impact assessment or a sustainability action proposal.

### E2.2 — Watersheds and water management/planning

Current Grade 8 expectations explicitly include demonstrating understanding of a watershed and explaining its importance to water management/planning.

Watershed Explorer candidate relations:

- runoff pathways;
- drainage relations;
- creek/Lake Ontario relation;
- model limitations;
- next-measurement selection;
- local stormwater-management sources.

**Strength:** STRONG_CANDIDATE_ALIGNMENT

**Gap:** the current physical model is intentionally simplified and does not yet require learners to define watershed boundaries from real topography.

### E2.6 — Water-quality indicators / human activity

Current Grade 8 expectations include water-quality indicators and human impacts.

Watershed Explorer candidate relations:

- optional field/sensor extension;
- runoff/pollution relation;
- Conservation Halton water-quality field relation.

**Strength:** PARTIAL_CANDIDATE_ALIGNMENT

**Gap:** the core 60-90 minute lesson does not itself measure real water quality. Do not claim this expectation is met without additional investigation.

### E2.7 — Municipal water processing / usage management

Current Grade 8 expectations include municipal water processing and water-use management.

Watershed Explorer candidate relations:

- municipal stormwater-management infrastructure;
- source links to Town of Oakville.

**Strength:** WEAK_PARTIAL_ALIGNMENT

**Gap:** stormwater management is not the same as municipal drinking-water/wastewater treatment. The module should not collapse these systems.

`STORMWATER_NE_DRINKING_WATER_TREATMENT`

Each cross-curricular claim requires its own current curriculum check.

## Accessibility / UDL relation

Ontario guidance describes Universal Design for Learning as flexible/supportive design that enables students to access curriculum as fully as possible.

Watershed Explorer already requires:

- meaningful non-headset path;
- seated path;
- large/projected text;
- captions where audiovisual;
- no critical information only in colour;
- motion-reduced option;
- safety/comfort stop without penalty.

R0B adds a review question that asks whether the **non-headset path preserves the same learning objective**, rather than merely existing.

## Review request

Educators should specifically challenge:

1. whether the proposed age/grade fit is realistic;
2. whether the scientific model creates misconceptions;
3. whether the inquiry is substantive or performative;
4. whether timing works in a real classroom;
5. whether the non-headset path is genuinely equivalent;
6. whether the rubric creates useful evidence without turning into unnecessary student scoring;
7. whether any stated curriculum relation is overstated.
