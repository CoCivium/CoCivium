# Local Outreach Pack

This pack is reusable copy for a future independent RoamLab operator. It is not proof of any partnership, endorsement or launch.

## 1. One-line public description

**A mobile immersive learning lab that brings the field trip before the field trip.**

## 2. Short public description

RoamLab brings curriculum-linked immersive learning to schools and communities using VR/AR, simulation, physical experiments and local evidence. Learners can prepare virtually, visit a real place, compare the model with reality, and build or explain what changed.

## 3. Teacher / principal introduction

Subject: A mobile immersive-learning pilot for Oakville schools

We are developing a mobile learning lab that can visit a school and run a curriculum-linked immersive session without requiring the school to own or manage a VR fleet.

The model is deliberately simple: students explore a simulation, form questions or predictions, then connect the experience to real evidence, local places or follow-up classroom work.

The first pilot is designed to operate while parked/on-site rather than transport students. It includes a non-headset participation path and does not require individual student accounts by default.

We are looking for educators willing to critique one early module and tell us what would make it genuinely useful in a classroom.

## 4. Tutoring / enrichment operator introduction

Subject: A portable immersive-learning sidecar for enrichment programs

RoamLab is an open business-plan and learning-module concept for adding immersive science, history, design and evidence-based exploration to an existing tutoring or enrichment business.

It can operate as a standalone program or as a sidecar to an existing brand. CoAll integration is optional. The open plan does not require equity, franchise fees or platform adoption.

A pilot can begin with a portable headset/display kit before any dedicated vehicle investment.

## 5. Local business sponsor introduction

Subject: Sponsor a real local learning experience

RoamLab is developing immersive, evidence-based learning sessions that can visit local schools and community programs.

A sponsor can fund seats, a school day, a module, equipment or a community session. Sponsor recognition is visible, but sponsorship does not buy learner data or control over educational conclusions.

Potential sector-linked examples include manufacturing, engineering, digital media, health/life sciences, professional services and sustainability.

The objective is straightforward: turn local expertise and resources into learning opportunities that remain useful even to people who never become CoAll users.

## 6. Destination / attraction introduction

Subject: Virtual preparation that sends learners into the real place better prepared

RoamLab is designed to complement rather than replace museums, parks, conservation areas and cultural sites.

A learner might first explore a model or reconstruction, generate questions, then visit the real destination and compare the model against primary sources, physical evidence, expert interpretation or field observations.

We are looking for destination operators willing to identify where this kind of pre-visit/post-visit layer would genuinely add value and where it would get in the way.

## 7. Sheridan / post-secondary collaboration introduction

Subject: Open immersive-learning pilot seeking critique, prototypes and student-project possibilities

RoamLab is an open mobile immersive-learning business-plan candidate focused on connecting virtual models to real local evidence and places.

Possible post-secondary relations include experiential-design prototypes, accessibility testing, game/interaction design, student capstones, internships, evaluation methods and open lesson assets.

This is an invitation to critique and experiment, not a claim of partnership.

## 8. Parent-facing description

RoamLab uses immersive technology as one tool inside a larger learning activity.

Children should not need persistent profiles, behavioural advertising or unnecessary recording. A non-headset alternative should be available. The facilitator should explain what data, if any, is collected and how it can be deleted or exported.

The objective is not more screen time for its own sake. It is to use simulation to help a learner ask better questions about the real world.

## 13. Evidence to carry into every sales conversation

Have ready:

- one live lesson;
- one 90-second demo;
- exact setup time;
- exact capacity;
- accessibility alternative;
- privacy sheet;
- safety sheet;
- pricing;
- teacher/facilitator guide;
- what happens when Wi-Fi fails;
- what happens when a headset fails;
- what data is collected;
- what is not collected;
- one concrete destination extension;
- one way the customer can use the module without CoAll.

## 14. Claims not to make

Do not say:

- "VR is proven to improve all learning";
- "Oakville/Town/Sheridan/Conservation Halton is our partner" without a real agreement;
- "the bus is a school";
- "the program is exempt from child-care law" without qualifying the real facts;
- "student data is anonymous" if re-identification is reasonably possible;
- "free" when mandatory paid services exist;
- "open source" for third-party content/hardware that is not actually open.

## 15. Local positioning rule

Local advertising should answer three questions quickly:

1. What does the learner actually do?
2. Why is this better than a cheaper video/classroom activity?
3. What happens after the headset comes off?

If the answer to number three is "nothing," the module is not ready.
