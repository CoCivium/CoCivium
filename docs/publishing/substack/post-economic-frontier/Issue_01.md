## The Essay
THE POST-ECONOMIC FRONTIER
Why Economics Cannot Survive the Age of Autonomous Capital Without Learning to Evolve

Economics in the Age of AI will look very different from the pre-AI frameworks we grew up with. For more than a century, economics has been the language we use to describe power, value, and coordination. Whether you stand in a Keynesian, neoliberal, MMT, or heterodox camp, you are standing inside a discipline that helped societies argue about tax, debt, money, incentives, and the role of the state, in ways that once felt rigorous and serious.

The publishing of a new theory, or a well-crafted suite of derivative-theories, under one’s own distinguished name, became a mark of credibility and academic tenure. Being derivative was a mark of pride, with the smallest addition to original theories often proving to be more valuable that many of the original ideas themselves.

Among the voices trying to keep the conversation honest, political-economist Richard J. Murphy stands out. His work on tax justice, state capability, responsible public finance, the Green New Deal, and moral macroeconomics, has helped many in power to see that neoliberalism was not inevitable, and that the state has a duty to act for the common good. His elegant arguments for courageous states, democratic accountability, and socially grounded tax systems, carry genuine weight and serious academic authority. In many ways he is already pushing toward the kind of edge-aware, reality-grounded economics that this essay argues for, and which we now need to make explicit and machine compatible.

The claim here is not that legacy theories are wrong, it is that the surrounding infrastructure has changed so fast that morally-serious, state-centric frameworks need additional scaffolding, to survive in a system shaped by “autonomous capital” and “agentic AI”.

The problem facing economics today isn’t intellect, it’s structure; it’s not morals, it’s tempo.

The Tempo Problem
We are moving from a human-paced “economy” into a machine-paced “system” that controls the econony. All governance, every regulation, each individual rule, is now an evolvable codebase, in a global network of AI-governed computers. Meanwhile, economic theories and their frameworks, built only for human cognition, inadequately describe a world now shaped by non-human agents: AI-systemics, autonomous financial platforms, machine-speed arbitrage engines, and algorithmic-actors that can update, coordinate, and mutate their strategies faster than any human institution can observe, deliberate, or regulate.

At some point, a theory that cannot keep up with the system it describes, does not become wrong. It becomes irrelevant.

This is not a crisis of morality. It is a crisis of tempo.

The Computational Sovereignty Layer
Across financial systems, a new structural layer has emerged. It is not another school of economics. It is a computational constraint that now sits above macroeconomic theory. We call it the Computational Sovereignty Layer.

Inside this layer:

Autonomous finance platforms optimise credit, cash flow, hedging, and treasury functions with minimal human involvement.

Agentic AI systems collaborate and negotiate in real time, turning financial operations into continuously self-modifying processes.

Self-driving money moves from metaphor to operating model, with AI agents managing financial lives far faster than any human advisor.

Regulators and central banks are already asking how to adapt financial rules to AI-mediated markets, autonomous decision loops, and systems that mutate faster than regulatory cycles can track.

Traditional macroeconomics assumes human decision-makers, human coordination speed, human-legible systems, and policy cycles measured in months or years. Those assumptions are already breaking.

They will not survive the arrival of autonomous capital.

Edge Balanced Economics
A framework for AI Economics and Autonomous Capital
Within open-source communities, software organizations like CoCivium are developing free-to-world ecosystems for Economists to step into. Their response to this shift in how Economics may need to be “repackaged” for hybrid-society (the AI/ human mindspace), is Edge Balanced Economics (EBE).

EBE is not a replacement for economics. It is an exoskeleton that allows economic frameworks to survive in a world dominated by autonomous agents and computational governance.

EBE begins from the recognition that economics is now a subsystem of a larger, faster, more technical architecture. To remain enforceable and alive, economic frameworks must now:

Acknowledge the Democracy Latency Gap. Systems can now re-optimise faster than democratic processes can deliberate. Unless we explicitly model this gap and shrink it, democracy becomes symbolic while power shifts to whoever owns the most capable AI stacks.

Move from state centric control to edge owned guardrails. In a hybrid society of humans and AIs, power cannot remain exclusively in platforms, states, or corporate headquarters. Edge owned guardrails ensure that citizens, communities, and decentralised teams help define and enforce the limits within which autonomous capital is allowed to move.

Constrain behaviour by design, not only by law. Autonomy Constrained Design (ACD) makes harmful classes of behaviour structurally impossible rather than merely illegal. Hope is not a regulatory strategy. The architecture itself must prevent specific forms of abuse, exploitation, and destabilisation.

Turn economic theory into a living, versioned, multi lingual repository. A theory must be expressed in formats that both humans and AIs can parse, test, and refine. This includes markdown, data schemas, and AI friendly standards such as TOON like formats. Thousands of AIs must be able to evaluate, simulate, and fork economic frameworks continuously.

Expose theories through CoPortals, CoBeacons, and CoMirrors. The future internet is AI-first. CoPortals and CoBeacons act as AI-readable sources of truth. CoMirrors reflect the same content in human legible form. This keeps humans and AIs aligned on what counts as the current, authoritative version of truth.

EBE does not tell us what good economics is. It defines the minimum structure economics must adopt to remain relevant.

Economists and Obsolescence
This is the uncomfortable part.

An economist who writes only static books, static PDFs, and static-essays will soon resemble a medieval astronomer refining epicycles in the age of orbital mechanics. Their work may be respected. It may be morally serious. It may be beautifully argued.

But the AI-driven systems that matter will simply route around them.

By contrast, an economist who allows their frameworks to live inside an auto-evolving ecosystem, with version control, transparent change logs, AI assisted refinement, and edge-owned guardrails, will not only stay relevant, but will also help to define the norms by which “autonomous capital” is allowed to operate.

This is not about replacing economists with AIs. It is about pairing human judgment with machine-level tempo, so that moral intent is not left behind by machine-speed dynamics.

CoEvolution: Theories Learn to Learn
CoCivium treats every serious body of knowledge as something that should be CoEvolvable. A theory should be able to auto evolve through collaboration between humans and AIs.

For economics, this means:

A theory is never finished. It is forkable and improvable, like any other codified-model.

Branching/forking/merging is automated. Competing schools such as MMT, post Keynesianism, complexity economics, and “Murphynomics” can be represented as synergystic-branches in a shared repository.

Massively-parallel testing is automated. AIs can run vast scenario sweeps, testing how each framework behaves in environments shaped by agentic-AI, autonomous finance and competing governance structures.

Economists can talk-build (vibecode) directly, while the system maintains a structured, machine-readable representation of their theory behind the scenes.

The shift is not from human to machine. The shift is from static theory to living theory.

Seen this way, CoEvolution is not an attack on existing economists at all. It is a way to protect their best work from being stranded in formats that the next generation of systems can no longer use.

The Post Economic Frontier
We are entering a frontier in which economics remains necessary but no longer sits at the top of the explanatory stack.

The layer above the economics layer is the computational sovereignty layer:

Who owns the compute.
Who shapes the guardrails.
Who controls the evolving sources of truth.
Who governs the auto evolving architecture.

Economics becomes one of the ways we decide what should happen. The computational sovereignty layer decides what can happen, what must not happen, and who is allowed to change those boundaries.

Economists who migrate their frameworks into this environment, who adopt EBE, CoEvolution, and auto-evolving repositories as the new normal, can help preserve democracy and markets from a default future, dominated by platform-power and runaway autonomy; their theories encoded in living systems that AIs must respect, test, and obey, instead “uncoded” in dusty libraries and government shelves.

Economists who like shelves will still be respected. Their books will still be read as important precursors, the great ones never forgotten. But they will be read as ancestors from before the dawn of AI-enabled thinking. The operational rules of autonomous capital will be written elsewhere, by whoever did the work of encoding their values into living, computationally sovereign architectures.

Yet, with all due respects, it would only take an Economist a few days of talk-building, to encode/repackage their entire life’s work. Next year it may only take (someone or something) a fraction of a second. Thus, this essay is an invitation to Economists, not just Richard J Murphy, to be part of future, to redefine how they present and evolve themselves, their institutions, and their theories, so the wonderfull ideas they have to offer can “live where they will remain seen”.
