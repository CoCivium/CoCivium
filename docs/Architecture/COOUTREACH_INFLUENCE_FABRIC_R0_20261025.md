# CoOutreach+ / Influence Fabric R0

**State:** PUBLIC_ARCHITECTURE_CANDIDATE__NOT_AUTOMATIC_OUTREACH__NOT_POLITICAL_PERSUASION_ENGINE__NOT_RUNTIME  
**Observed:** 2026-10-25

## Thesis

Large-scale parallel publication and outreach can create real-world effects.

Those effects must be typed by domain, receiver, authority, consent, evidence and risk.

```
REACH
-> ATTENTION
-> INTERPRETATION
-> RESPONSE
-> USE / REJECTION / CHALLENGE
-> POSSIBLE INFLUENCE
```

Influence is an observed outcome, not an entitlement or an automatic objective.

## Influence classes

### Commercial
Allowed candidate goals:
- product awareness;
- education;
- opt-in lead generation;
- transparent offers;
- partner discovery;
- developer adoption;
- customer support;
- market research.

Requirements:
- truthful claims;
- clear sender identity;
- easy opt-out;
- no deceptive scarcity;
- no hidden affiliation;
- privacy-respecting targeting;
- no spam-like fanout.

### Research / scholarly
Allowed candidate goals:
- invite critique;
- circulate preprints / essays / methods;
- request replication;
- recruit collaborators;
- surface counterarguments.

Requirements:
- source provenance;
- uncertainty;
- no implied endorsement;
- no affiliation inflation;
- citation and challenge path.

### Cultural / artistic
Allowed candidate goals:
- share stories, media, diagrams, humour and speculative work;
- invite interpretation / remix / discussion;
- build communities around ideas.

Requirements:
- distinguish fiction, metaphor, hypothesis and empirical claim;
- preserve rights/attribution;
- avoid covert manipulation.

### Theological / philosophical
Allowed candidate goals:
- publish questions, reflections and comparative frameworks;
- invite dialogue across traditions;
- support believer / doubter / other-tradition readability.

Requirements:
- no claimed divine authority;
- no fabricated revelation or receiver response;
- no coercive conversion tactics;
- explicit distinction between belief, metaphor, philosophy and evidence.

### Civic / political
Allowed candidate goals for the CoAll public-interest lane:
- nonpartisan civic information;
- source-bound policy/process explanation;
- election-security / civic-resilience education;
- publication of clearly attributed viewpoints;
- inviting plural critique and public-interest research;
- encouraging lawful participation in general terms.

Not a system objective:
- targeted political persuasion;
- voter manipulation;
- covert ideological profiling;
- microtargeted pressure;
- fabricated grassroots support;
- impersonation;
- automated harassment;
- suppressing turnout or participation.

`CIVIC_INFORMATION_NE_PARTISAN_PERSUASION`

### Community / humanitarian
Allowed candidate goals:
- coordination;
- mutual aid discovery;
- accessibility;
- education;
- emergency/resilience information;
- volunteer / donor information when transparent and lawful.

## Parallelism model

Use massive **logical** parallelism for:
- research;
- receiver mapping;
- message variants;
- translation;
- visual/media variants;
- channel-fit analysis;
- rights review;
- fact checks;
- counterarguments;
- accessibility;
- scheduling proposals;
- measurement design.

Use bounded physical execution for:
- publication;
- direct sends;
- paid promotion;
- public campaigns;
- consequential external actions.

`MASSIVE_PARALLEL_RESEARCH_NE_MASS_BLAST`

## Receiver ladder

```
DISCOVERABLE
-> ROUTED
-> SENT / PUBLISHED
-> DELIVERED
-> OBSERVED
-> ACKNOWLEDGED
-> UNDERSTOOD
-> USED
-> CHALLENGED
-> CONTRIBUTED
-> PROPAGATED
```

Never infer higher rungs from lower ones.

`ENGAGEMENT_NE_VALUE`
`VIRALITY_NE_VALIDITY`

## Cross-domain contamination guard

A commercial, theological, research or political lane must not silently borrow authority from another lane.

Examples:
- research citation does not imply political endorsement;
- theological interest does not imply commercial consent;
- customer status does not imply civic targeting permission;
- public comment does not imply direct-message permission.

`RELATION_NE_PERMISSION_TRANSFER`

## Election / civic-period resilience

During election periods:
- prioritize source verification;
- preserve timestamps;
- resist impersonation and scams;
- distinguish rumor from observation;
- avoid unverified amplification;
- correct visibly;
- keep civic material nonpartisan unless publishing a clearly attributed human viewpoint;
- avoid individualized political persuasion or profiling.

`SCENARIO_NE_FORECAST`
`ELECTION_TIMING_NE_PREDICTED_UNREST`

## First proof

Take one Great ReFact chapter and compile six projections:
1. Substack essay;
2. developer/GitHub explainer;
3. short video;
4. press brief;
5. commercial partner explainer;
6. nonpartisan civic-resilience note.

For each, record audience, changed framing, unchanged factual kernel, authority, excluded claims, and receiver action.

Success means one source can travel widely without collapsing domain-specific ethics, consent or evidence.
