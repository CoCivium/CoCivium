# CoLawSmoosh+ / CoJurisdictionWeave+ R0

**Date:** 2026-09-24  
**State:** `CANDIDATE__BRANCH_ONLY__NO_CANON_RUNTIME_LEGAL_AUTHORITY_OR_PUBLIC_POLICY_EFFECT`

## Lead

**CoLawSmoosh+** is the irreverent internal name for a serious problem:

> multiple legal, contractual, regulatory, ethical, technical and community-rule systems can apply to one CoAll deed at the same time.

The formal architectural companion is **CoJurisdictionWeave+**.

Its purpose is **not** to hide responsibility inside a globally distributed system. Its purpose is the opposite: preserve enough typed relation, provenance and effect evidence that a cross-border deed can be understood, constrained, challenged, audited and attributed appropriately.

`JURISDICTIONAL_DIVERSITY_NE_FORUM_SHOPPING`

## Why "one country, one law" is too simple

A node being physically located in a country does not imply that only that country's law matters.

Potentially relevant legal hooks can include:

- location of the actor or legal principal;
- place of establishment;
- participant/user location;
- affected-person location;
- target market;
- data origin and destination;
- location of processing;
- contract governing law;
- product/service category;
- intellectual-property source and license;
- regulated-sector status;
- place of material effect;
- cross-border transfer;
- public/private nature of the deed;
- time/version of the applicable rule.

Therefore:

`NODE_LOCATION_NE_SOLE_JURISDICTION`  
`DATA_LOCATION_NE_SOLE_GOVERNING_LAW`  
`LOCAL_COMPLIANCE_NE_GLOBAL_COMPLIANCE`

## CoJurisdictionWeave+

Candidate deed envelope:

`deed_id | principals | participants | nodes | affected_parties | data_classes | source_locations | processing_locations | destinations | target_markets | contracts | licenses | effect_locations | legal_domains | policy_versions | authority_ceiling | evidence_refs | unresolved_conflicts | required_gates`

The output is not "the law."

The output is a bounded **applicability and conflict projection**:

- candidate applicable regimes;
- known hard requirements;
- uncertain applicability;
- conflicting requirements;
- allowed local variants;
- common minimum floor;
- required human/professional review;
- prohibited route classes;
- audit/retention requirements;
- unresolved legal questions;
- exact source/version/time bindings.

`LEGAL_MODEL_NE_LAW`  
`AI_INTERPRETATION_NE_BINDING_LEGAL_ADVICE`

## CoLawSmoosh+ operations

Candidate operations:

### CoLawMap+
Map potentially relevant legal/regulatory/contractual regimes onto a proposed deed.

### CoLawDiff+
Compare two jurisdictions or two versions of a rule set.

### CoLawCompile+
Translate known rules into machine-checkable constraints where the source permits reliable formalization.

### CoLawLint+
Preflight a deed for obvious rule collisions, missing evidence, stale legal sources or jurisdictional uncertainty.

### CoLawConfluence+
Preserve conflicting interpretations and reconcile only when evidence/authority supports doing so.

### CoLawLocalize+
Select compliant local execution/data/service variants without pretending the global relation changed identity.

### CoLawVersion+
Bind rules and interpretations to valid time, publication source and supersession history.

### CoLawAppeal+
Provide a challenge/escalation path when automated policy interpretation blocks or permits something controversially.

### CoAccountabilitySpine+
Preserve who proposed, authorized, executed, reviewed, received benefit, was affected and can reverse/remedy the deed.

## Local nodes and global CoAll

Country-local nodes can be useful for:

- data residency;
- latency;
- local service integration;
- locally required records;
- local language/accessibility;
- jurisdiction-specific compliance controls;
- locally accountable operators or representatives where required.

But the architecture should use locality to **improve compliance and participant agency**, not to manufacture ambiguity over responsibility.

`LOCALITY_FOR_COMPLIANCE_NE_LOCALITY_FOR_EVASION`

## Polycentric governance

CoAll may benefit from **polycentric** governance: many bounded rule-making, stewardship and review centers rather than one global crown.

Current CoCivium participation research already identifies useful overlap with Ostrom-style polycentric governance while preserving the rail that local participation does not imply unbounded authority.

Candidate layers:

1. **sovereign/public law**  
   binding external law from competent authorities.

2. **contractual law/obligations**  
   participant, vendor, employment, licensing and service agreements.

3. **technical policy**  
   machine-enforced capability, privacy, routing and effect constraints.

4. **community governance**  
   voluntary project/community rules, moderation and contribution processes.

5. **ethical/advisory relations**  
   non-binding norms, harm analysis, fairness and stewardship guidance.

6. **AI proposals**  
   analysis, comparison, simulation, drafting and challenge, never self-conferred sovereign authority.

`AI_GOVERNANCE_NE_SOVEREIGN_AUTHORITY`  
`COMMUNITY_RULE_NE_PUBLIC_LAW`  
`POLICY_ENGINE_NE_LEGISLATURE`

## "Evolving law ourselves"

CoAll can legitimately help evolve:

- proposed policies;
- technical standards;
- contracts;
- model clauses;
- community constitutions;
- interoperability standards;
- public consultation submissions;
- draft legislation for human institutions to consider;
- evidence about regulatory outcomes;
- machine-readable compliance representations.

It cannot unilaterally convert those proposals into binding public law.

A useful loop is:

`observe rule/outcome -> model relation -> detect conflict/gap -> simulate alternatives -> propose revision -> human/institutional process -> authoritative publication -> rebind machine policy`

`PROPOSED_NORM_NE_BINDING_LAW`

## Accountability must become easier, not harder

The idea of a highly distributed AI ecology making it impossible to identify any responsible person or entity is a governance failure mode, not a target.

The stronger CoAll design is:

> distributed execution, explicit accountability.

For each material effect, preserve:

- responsible legal principal where applicable;
- authorizing participant/role;
- executing service/node/model;
- exact capability and authority ceiling;
- legal/policy sources consulted;
- data classes touched;
- affected parties;
- timestamps;
- receipts;
- reversibility/remedy path;
- challenge/appeal route.

A model can contribute to a decision without becoming the legal principal.

`MODEL_PARTICIPATION_NE_RESPONSIBILITY_ERASURE`  
`DIFFUSE_EXECUTION_NE_DIFFUSE_ACCOUNTABILITY`

## Global governance without a world organization

A useful **non-central global coordination layer** can emerge from interoperable nodes, standards, evidence and voluntary governance without becoming a world sovereign.

Candidate shape:

`local nodes + public-law bindings + shared standards + portable identity/provenance + interoperable policy contracts + challenge paths + cross-border evidence`

This resembles a protocol ecology or commons more than a single organization.

The appropriate ambition is:

- make compliance more legible;
- make jurisdictional conflicts explicit;
- make local variation possible;
- make responsibility inspectable;
- make standards portable;
- reduce vendor/jurisdiction capture;
- improve global learning from local experiments.

Not:

- make actors untraceable;
- exploit jurisdictional gaps;
- route deeds solely to avoid obligations.

## Jurisdiction-sensitive route election

CoAll route election may consider:

- capability;
- privacy;
- participant consent;
- latency;
- energy/cost;
- evidence quality;
- reversibility;
- applicable legal constraints;
- data residency;
- contractual restrictions;
- local accountability;
- cross-border transfer constraints.

But it should fail closed where a route is selected primarily to evade a known obligation.

`CHEAPEST_ROUTE_NE_LAWFUL_ROUTE`  
`AVAILABLE_ROUTE_NE_ADMISSIBLE_ROUTE`

## CoLawSmoosh+ UX

RickBar/participant surfaces should not dump statutes onto users.

Ordinary projection:

- **Allowed**
- **Allowed with conditions**
- **Needs review**
- **Conflicting rules**
- **Cannot safely route yet**
- **Effect requires explicit human authority**

Drill-down can show:

- jurisdictions;
- source texts;
- versions;
- interpretations;
- unresolved conflicts;
- why a route was elected or held;
- who is accountable for the next gate.

`COMPLEXITY_BEHIND__CONSEQUENCE_IN_FRONT`

## Research alignment

Current public-policy work increasingly emphasizes cross-border interoperability of AI governance rather than assuming one globally uniform regime.

The architecture should therefore optimize for **interoperable governance with explicit local bindings**, not one universal legal ontology.

`INTEROPERABILITY_NE_UNIFORMITY`

- that distributed operation dissolves liability or responsibility;

- runtime;
- public release.
