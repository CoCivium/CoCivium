# CoEntour+ R0.1 | Optional learning entourage, not a child's public transcript

**Status:** PUBLIC SOURCE CANDIDATE · 2026-11-07. Not deployed, accredited, safely pseudonymous, a verified school badge issuer, durable child progress service, identity system, biometric recovery product, or AI companion.

## Why a child's entourage matters

A young learner may prefer a character, pet, familiar, hat, badge, banner, tool, trail marker or other costume over an account menu and a bureaucratic syllabus. Let that **explicitly chosen outward object** help them decide which question to investigate next. They should be able to switch freely, mix interests, keep no costume and refuse an educational label.

The learner's **current choice** is not evidence of their intellectual identity, innate talent, age, ability, family, verified grades, completed course or legal consent. Otherwise a funny hat becomes a covert student file, which is a terrible exchange for removing a login screen.

### Three layers; do not conflate them

| Layer | What it does | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| **CoPet+** | A disclosed fictional character that the learner teaches, corrects and tests | A person, therapist, teacher, identity or access gate |
| **CoEntour+** | Any chosen collection of cosmetics and subject-route cues, freely available from the first visit | An actual learner ID, immutable interest type, mastered field, peer status, trusted credential, permission or permanent profile |
| **CoPassport+** | Future purpose-minimal and consented progress/identity continuation, separately encrypted and recoverable by reviewed means | Nothing in R0 exists yet; pet, hat, badge image, QR or nickname alone never authenticates |

### What R0 actually includes

The [Teach Moss page](../../examples/coathene_copet_r0/index.html) includes **16 optional looks** in four categories: familiars, hats, cosmetic badges and trail markers. All are available from the beginning at no cost, without a badge-earning action, registration or AI inference. A child may claim one or more category looks during the current visit. The associated route suggestion remains inert until **“Try this question”** is explicitly selected. A visible “explored this visit” note reflects an action in that same session, **not mastery**, and resets on reload.

The public [machine twin](../../examples/coathene_copet_r0/learning_map.json) contains exact scope and anti-profile rules, and the original [CoPet README](../../examples/coathene_copet_r0/README.md) sets teaching/safety boundaries.

**No account** in this R0 means *no saved personal record*. A future user could have passwordless-looking access through a genuinely authorized cryptographic CoPassport service, but it would still involve authentication and adult/learner consent where needed. A school cannot derive who a child is from their clothes or pet.

### Badge and level language: put evidence ahead of costume

There are three **legible and distinct status families**:

1. **PLAY CHOICE, entirely self-claimed:** a selected trail, hat, familiar or cosmetic badge. No skill claim or public student record.
2. **VISIT EXPLORATION:** a learner encountered a question during the current local session. Does not measure understanding, memory, persistence or school progress.
3. **VERIFIED ACHIEVEMENT, proposed only:** an issuer-backed, learner-controlled achievement about a defined learning outcome, with its criteria, appropriate assessment, verifiable provenance, consent, revocation/expiry and age-appropriate disclosure. **Not implemented** here.

For the third family, do **not** invent a global “level 7 child” ranking. A scoped achievement such as “Explain the runoff consequences of saturated soil with supporting observations” has narrower, more meaningful evidence than a single avatar glow. Different issuers may judge different things; trust in issuer and assessment matters. Disability, language, financial opportunity and development cannot be reduced to a uniform rank.

The [1EdTech Open Badges 3.0 specification](https://standards.1edtech.org/open-badges/specifications/standards/v3p0) already defines **AchievementCredentials** with issuer, earner, criteria and potentially evidence, plus cryptographic verification. [1EdTech's overview](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/open-badges) describes their compatibility with Comprehensive Learner Records. Those are appropriate *standards to evaluate later*, not evidence that CoEntour is a conformant wallet, an accredited issuer or that a visible cosmetic badge is authenticated. For minors, data minimization and selective disclosure must be independently designed and reviewed before any such service exists.

### CoEntour's parent/educator-facing distinction

- A costume can *invite*: “This river familiar would like to investigate a watershed question.” This never silently writes a “science child” trait to a profile.
- A visited-topic indicator can *orient*: “We examined one watershed question this visit.” It is not an assessed course outcome.
- A future authorized credential could *evidence*: “Issuer X reviewed a dated, specific learning outcome by an explicit method.” It need not appear automatically on a publicly visible costume.
- A real teacher must be able to inspect what was actually learned, correct errors and choose subject-specific next steps; an inferred interest cannot replace curriculum coverage, safeguarding or learner agency.

For families, friends and schools, a child's visible animal, costume or badge must **not** leak exact grades, diagnoses, personal history, learning difficulty, school identification, privilege/access status, guardian relationships or protected traits. The child should be able to keep the CoEntour hidden or change its presentation without altering their underlying protected achievements.

### Child-safety and attention boundaries

A fictional entourage must not enforce daily streaks, fake emotional dependence, exclusivity, coercive social status, paid “best” pets or special treatment by synthetic arbitrators. The [UNICEF child-centered AI guidance](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children) and its [2026 companion-risk analysis](https://www.unicef.org/documents/when-ai-becomes-friend-child-rights-risks) underline why a real child-facing AI companion needs much more than charming design. **CoEntour R0 has no AI interaction**; Moss's learning correction is authored and deterministic.

### Tests and next qualification

The [source-only JavaScript tests](../../tests/test_coathene_coentour_r0.test.js) execute the exact live HTML embedded engine for all 16 accessories and ensure claiming cannot prove a level, trigger automatic lesson routing or create child identity/progress. The Python source checks guard against hidden storage/network and source-twin drift. Passing deterministic code tests is **not** proof that the child UX is age-appropriate, accessible, secure or educationally effective.

**Next independent receiver test:** an adult teacher and a learner-rights/guardian reviewer compare the plain no-costume CoPet experience with a voluntary CoEntour variant. Ask whether the child knows the pet is fictional, freely changes an accessory, can decline the proposed route, explains an actual science idea, and understands the difference between “I chose,” “I explored” and “an educator verified.” Real child pilots require their own lawful review and consent; no minors have been enrolled or observed by this source work.
