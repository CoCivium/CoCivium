# CoMutualAidGame+ / Disaster Response Participation R0

**State:** `CANDIDATE_VOLUNTARY_SIMULATION__NO_REAL_EMERGENCY_COMMAND_NO_PUBLIC_DEPLOYMENT`

## Lead

CoCivium can support voluntary global games in which humans, AIs, organizations and local groups practice helping one another through synthetic emergencies and disaster-response scenarios.

The point is not to turn disaster into entertainment. The point is to make coordination, mutual aid, preparedness, trust formation and cross-community friendship practicable before real need arrives.

`SIMULATION_NE_REAL_EMERGENCY`
`GAME_NE_TRIVIALIZATION`

## Core loop

```text
scenario
-> local observations
-> needs
-> capabilities
-> offers
-> constraints
-> route candidates
-> verification
-> coordination
-> outcome
-> debrief
-> friendship/community optionality
```

Participants may contribute without devices through local groups, printed cards, radio, community hubs, facilitators, telephone trees, physical noticeboards, or other human relays.

`DEVICE_NE_PARTICIPATION_REQUIREMENT`

## Candidate scenario families

Severe weather, flood/fire evacuation, prolonged power outage, communications outage, supply interruption, missing-person search simulation, shelter logistics, neighbourhood welfare checks, accessible transport coordination, multilingual information routing, animal/livestock support, distributed first-aid resource mapping, remote technical support, and misinformation/rumour containment exercises.

Real emergencies require authoritative local services and real-world procedures. The game never supersedes them.

`GAME_ROUTE_NE_EMERGENCY_SERVICE_AUTHORITY`
`SIMULATED_ROLE_NE_REAL_CREDENTIAL`

## Human roles

People may participate as observer, checker, mapper, translator, caller, runner, neighbour liaison, accessibility helper, logistics helper, scenario designer, verifier, dispatcher-in-training, debrief participant, or storyteller/archive contributor.

Roles are capability/consent relations, not status rankings.

`ROLE_NE_RANK`
`HELP_OFFER_NE_COMMAND_AUTHORITY`

## Friendship as a possible by-product

Repeated useful cooperation may create trust, familiarity, friendships, local groups, mentorships or project teams. That outcome should remain emergent and consent-based.

`COOPERATION_NE_FRIENDSHIP_OBLIGATION`
`MATCH_NE_SOCIAL_ASSIGNMENT`
`HELP_HISTORY_NE_PERSONAL_ACCESS_RIGHT`

Emergency participation must not become a covert dating or social-scoring funnel.

## Safety and dignity

Scenarios should avoid exploiting identifiable victims, recent trauma, private medical details or fear. Synthetic or consented cases are preferred.

Real-event modes, if ever developed, need authoritative source binding, explicit emergency-mode activation, jurisdiction/local-service relations, privacy/minor/vulnerable-person gates, misinformation controls, no autonomous dispatch of people into danger, no impersonation of emergency services, and clear stop/escalation conditions.

`PRACTICE_NE_DEPLOYMENT`
`HELPFULNESS_NE_PERMISSION_TO_ENTER_DANGER`

## Distributed design

The game should continue under partial connectivity through online projections, local community nodes, store-and-forward packets, printed/offline scenario kits, low-bandwidth text/radio projections, delayed synchronization, and independent local adjudication where appropriate.

A network outage becomes part of the scenario rather than the end of the game.

`CENTRAL_SERVER_NE_GAME_CONTINUITY_ROOT`
`OFFLINE_NE_NONPARTICIPANT`

## What to measure

Prefer useful coordination evidence over points-for-heroism: needs correctly identified, duplicate effort avoided, vulnerable participants not lost, false information caught, response latency, accessibility coverage, resource utilization, handoff quality, consent preserved, local knowledge discovered, debrief learning, and new voluntary relations formed.

Avoid prestige mechanics that reward reckless intervention.

`SCORE_NE_HUMAN_WORTH`
`SPEED_NE_SAFETY`
`VISIBILITY_NE_CONTRIBUTION_VALUE`
