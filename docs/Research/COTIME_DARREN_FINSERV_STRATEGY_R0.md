## CoTime+ lens

CoTime+ distinguishes:

- source-time;
- observation-time;
- currentness;
- prediction;
- supersession;
- parallel temporal branches.

That matters in financial services because a useful forecast is not just a number. It is a dated model applied to a particular information state.

A useful object can therefore be:

forecast_time | information_cutoff | model_version | assumptions | target | confidence | scenario | observed_outcome | calibration_result

This allows later review of:

what was knowable then,
what was inferred then,
what changed,
what the forecast missed,
and whether the model improved.

## Why CoTime+ can change business models

A conventional professional-services model often sells a human process:

input -> analysis -> recommendation -> client communication -> execution -> periodic review

A more AI-native model may become:

continuous evidence -> continuous currentness -> multiple bounded analytical lanes -> challenge -> scenario generation -> client-specific explanation -> decision gate -> monitoring -> recalibration

Potential business-model pressure points:

- less value in purely repetitive research;
- faster client-response expectations;
- more continuous rather than periodic review;
- greater value in judgment, trust, explanation and accountability;
- more intense need for documented model/input provenance;
- lower cost for some back-office analysis;
- higher importance of conflict-of-interest controls when systems optimize interactions;
- more demand for portable client data and vendor independence;
- potential compression of fees for commoditized analysis;
- new premium value for high-trust fiduciary/client relationships and complex decisions.

These are scenarios, not predictions.

## Client-account strategy: protective first

For any real client accounts, a prudent AI-transition strategy should begin with controls rather than speculative trading.

### 1. Inventory

For each workflow classify:

- data touched;
- decision affected;
- model/tool used;
- external provider;
- sensitivity;
- legal/contractual constraints;
- human gate;
- reversibility;
- current evidence.

### 2. Separate observation from advice

An AI system may summarize or flag something without being treated as the decision maker.

Candidate relations:

OBSERVED_BY
ANALYZED_BY
PROPOSED_BY
REVIEWED_BY
AUTHORIZED_BY
EXECUTED_BY

### 3. Build a shadow lane before changing client outcomes

Run selected AI-derived observations beside current human practice.

Measure:

- accuracy;
- omissions;
- false positives;
- false negatives;
- latency;
- consistency;
- calibration;
- client-impact risk;
- conflict-of-interest risk.

Do not let a model silently change actual client allocations merely because it looks impressive.

### 4. Keep client portability

Avoid allowing the client relationship or important client records to become trapped inside a single AI vendor.

Candidate controls:

- exportability;
- local/archive copies where permitted;
- provenance;
- provider change plan;
- contract review;
- data minimization.

### 5. Reassess continuously

CoTime+ means a 2026 policy may be outdated in 2027 even when the underlying business has not changed.

Use:

policy_version | effective_time | reviewed_at | supersedes | trigger_for_reassessment

## SEC relationals

The SEC has explicitly examined predictive data analytics and AI in investor interactions.

Important current-status fact:

In June 2025 the SEC withdrew its 2023 proposed rules on conflicts of interest associated with predictive data analytics and related proposals rather than adopting those proposals as final rules. The SEC's current rulemaking index still records S7-12-23 as a withdrawal.

Therefore:

Do not describe the 2023 proposal as current binding law.

At the same time, the SEC's 2023 materials are useful evidence of a regulatory concern: technology that predicts, guides, forecasts or directs investor behavior can create conflicts when a firm's interests are optimized alongside or ahead of investors' interests.

Current compliance analysis therefore belongs in the existing rules and facts of the particular firm, role, service and technology rather than in a claim that a withdrawn proposal is currently applicable.

Primary sources:

https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/06/s7-12-23
https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/rulemaking-activity
https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-140

## Marketing / communication relation

SEC investment-adviser marketing guidance identifies restrictions around materially misleading advertising, testimonials/endorsements, performance presentation and hypothetical performance.

For any Darren-facing or client-facing material, maintain a separate review lane for:

## Strategy scenarios for Darren

### Scenario A: augmentation

AI reduces routine workload while Darren remains responsible for judgment and client relationship.

Potential effects:

- capacity increases;
- response times shorten;
- more time for complex clients;
- margins may change.

### Scenario B: partial commoditization

Common analysis becomes widely reproducible.

Potential effects:

- clients expect lower-cost research;
- differentiation shifts toward trust, context, implementation and accountability;
- repetitive analytical services face pricing pressure.

### Scenario C: continuous advisory

Clients expect current, monitored, personalized information rather than periodic meetings.

Potential effects:

- service cadence changes;
- workflows need currentness controls;
- staff roles become more supervisory/interpretive.

### Scenario D: prediction becomes reflexive

If many actors act on similar forecasts, forecasts can change the environment being forecast.

Potential effects:

- model outputs feed back into markets and client behavior;
- calibration becomes more important;
- identical forecasts can become less informative;
- proprietary process quality may matter less than adaptation and diversified information.

### Scenario E: platform substitution

Open-source and interoperable tools reduce dependence on closed software vendors.

Potential effects:

- more portable workflows;
- lower switching costs;
- new sidecar products;
- greater pressure on incumbent platform margins;
- stronger need to maintain data and relationship portability.

### Scenario F: regulatory complexity

Local and cross-border rules proliferate or change at different speeds.

Potential effects:

- compliance becomes a versioned knowledge problem;
- jurisdiction-aware routing becomes more valuable;
- human/legal review becomes a high-value exception path.
